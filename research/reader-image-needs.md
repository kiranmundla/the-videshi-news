# Reader Image Needs Analysis — The Videshi
**Date:** 2026-10-11
**Sample:** 30 most recent published articles across all categories
**Lens:** What does the READER need? What makes them stop, trust, share?

---

## 1. Reader Image Needs by Article Type

### Named Person Stories (47% of articles)
**Reader question:** "Is this really them? Do I recognize them?"
**Emotional hooks:** Recognition, nostalgia, pride, aspiration
**What works:** Recent, high-quality photos from publishers (crictracker, indianexpress, moneycontrol)
**What fails:** Decade-old Wikimedia photos (the Himesh Reshammiya problem)
**Reader need:** A photo that matches their mental image of this person TODAY

### Immigration Stories (highest reader stakes)
**Reader question:** "Does this affect MY visa? Should I worry?"
**Emotional hooks:** URGENCY, anxiety, hope
**Current state:** WEAKEST images. Green card freeze article uses generic Pexels stock.
**Reader need:** Images that convey gravity and officialdom — not cheerful stock photos.
**Gap:** No source currently provides immigration-specific imagery.

### Government/Policy (14%)
**Reader question:** "What is the government actually doing?"
**Emotional hooks:** Civic engagement, national pride, accountability
**Current state:** 57% use generic Wikimedia. Adequate but not compelling.
**Reader need:** Real photos from the actual event/policy announcement.

### Festival/Culture
**Reader question:** "This is MY festival — does it feel like it?"
**Emotional hooks:** Joy, nostalgia, cultural pride, belonging
**Current state:** Generic Wikimedia images. Topically correct but emotionally flat.
**Reader need:** VIBRANT, celebratory images that make diaspora readers feel connected to home.
**Gap:** No source captures the emotional vibrancy of festivals. Stock diyas don't make someone feel Navratri.

### Food Stories (6+ articles in sample)
**Reader question:** "Does this look delicious? Would I eat this?"
**Emotional hooks:** Craving, nostalgia (taste of home), curiosity
**What works:** Publisher food photography (indianexpress, curlytales), brand official (Mother's Recipe)
**Reader need:** Appetizing, professional food photography. Amateur bowl photos kill appetite appeal.

### Travel/Destination (9%)
**Reader question:** "Should I visit? Is it beautiful?"
**Emotional hooks:** Wanderlust, aspiration, nostalgia
**Current state:** Mixed. Some good Wikimedia, some generic.
**Reader need:** Breathtaking destination photography that triggers "I need to go there."

### Markets/Finance
**Reader question:** "Should I invest? What does this mean for my money?"
**Emotional hooks:** Opportunity, anxiety about wealth
**Current state:** Moneycontrol graphics, Wikimedia generic. Informative but not emotional.
**Reader need:** Conceptual images that convey economic mood — hard to source well.

### Entertainment/Trailers
**Reader question:** "Should I watch this?"
**Emotional hooks:** Excitement, fandom
**What works:** YouTube maxres thumbnails (1280×720). Already good.
**Reader need:** High-energy trailer stills. We do this well.

## 2. Gap Analysis: Where We Fall Short for Readers

### 🔴 Critical Gap: Immigration Imagery
Immigration is our highest-stakes category — readers' lives literally depend on visa policy. But we use generic Pexels stock photos. A reader worried about their green card freeze doesn't connect with a smiling stock photo of an office.
**What's needed:** Official, grave, institutional imagery that matches the emotional weight.

### 🟡 Major Gap: Festival Emotional Vibrancy
Diaspora readers experience festivals as deep emotional connections to home. Our generic Wikimedia diya photos are topically correct but emotionally flat. They don't make someone in New Jersey FEEL Diwali.
**What's needed:** Vibrant, high-energy celebration photography — people dancing, colors, lights, joy.

### 🟡 Major Gap: Diaspora-Specific Imagery
No free source captures Indian-American/Canadian/British life — Diwali in Edison, Navratri in Houston, Holi in London. Our readers live this; our images don't show it.
**What's needed:** Diaspora community event photography (copyright-clean).

### 🟢 Minor Gap: Conceptual Finance Imagery
Markets stories need images that convey economic mood. Current sources are adequate but uninspired.

## 3. Resource Recommendations

### NEW DISCOVERY: DHS Public Domain Multimedia ⭐
**Source:** dhs.gov/photo-video-audio-use-guidelines
**Copyright:** CONFIRMED public domain — "imagery, video, and audio materials produced for and by DHS are considered public domain and not copyrighted within the United States"
**Why it matters:** DHS includes USCIS, ICE, CBP — ALL the immigration agencies. This directly fills our #1 critical gap (immigration imagery).
**Use case:** Green card, H-1B, visa policy, deportation, border stories
**Attribution:** "U.S. Department of Homeland Security"
**Action:** Add DHS multimedia search to the pipeline for immigration-category articles. VALIDATED — build recommended.

### Festival Vibrancy: Openverse with Emotion Keywords
**Why:** Openverse (800M CC images, no key) can be queried with emotional terms, not just topical ones. Instead of searching "Diwali," search "Diwali celebration joy" or "Navratri dance crowd."
**Action:** Enhance Openverse queries with emotional modifiers for festival/culture articles.

### Diaspora Imagery: Strategic Gap
No free source exists for diaspora community photography. Options:
1. **Partnership approach:** Reach out to major diaspora orgs (FIA, etc.) for photo-sharing agreements — they were rejected for scraping, but a partnership is different from scraping.
2. **Reader submissions:** "Share your Diwali" — user-generated content with permission. High engagement + free images.
3. **Event photographer credits:** Offer photo credit + linkback to diaspora event photographers in exchange for usage rights.
**Action:** Kiran decision — this needs a relationship/partnership approach, not a technical one.

### Food: Already Improving
Brand-first sourcing (built) + publisher og:images are working. No new resource needed.

## Key Insight

The pipeline has been optimized for TECHNICAL quality (resolution, source reputation). But readers don't care about resolution — they care about EMOTIONAL resonance. A 1920px generic diya photo doesn't beat a 800px photo of actual people celebrating.

**The next frontier isn't more sources — it's more EMOTIONAL sources.**
