# Reel Quality & Reliability Strategy — The Videshi

**Date:** 2026-10-12 · **Status:** For Kiran's approval — no implementation in this document
**Scope:** The entire reel operation: generation, QA, registration, distribution, governance.
**Trigger:** A public Short with edge-cropped text and a wrong on-screen year (Oct 12), plus the discovery that 8 unapproved Shorts were auto-published Oct 9–12.

---

## Executive summary

The reel operation does not have a quality problem at the edges. It has three structural problems, each proven by evidence below:

1. **The live generation path runs no QA at all, and the database lies about it.** Quick Pulse — the only format produced in October — never calls any QA function. `register_reel()` in `shotstack-reel.py` hardcodes `"qa_passed": True` and defaults `qa_score` to the literal number **8**. Every one of the 12 Pulse reels registered Oct 9–12 carries `qa_score = 8`. The score is not measured; it is typed into the code.
2. **Publishing was never actually gated.** A legacy daily job uploaded any MP4 it found in `pipeline/reels/` to YouTube as **public**, selecting by its own "importance score," with no reference to `prebuilt_reels`, QA, or approval — and no write-back. It published 8 Shorts Oct 9–12, including one with cropped text and one whose on-screen text stated the wrong election year. Two ledgers of "what is live" (`prebuilt_reels` vs `youtube-log.json`) disagree, and no process reconciles them. The breach was discovered by the channel owner watching YouTube, not by any monitor.
3. **Text layout is never measured before rendering.** Scene text is generated by an LLM and handed to Shotstack's remote HTML/CSS renderer at fixed font sizes. Whether a line fits the frame is discovered — if at all — after the video is public. This class was "fixed" three times in a single day (Oct 10: commits `2d3330c7`, `c4e50473`, `b1e1cdd6`) and recurred on Oct 12, because each fix tuned one instance (e.g., caption font 42px → 36px) instead of introducing measurement.

**Recommendation in one line:** rebuild Pulse on a **local, deterministic renderer** (PIL + ffmpeg — both already proven in this codebase) where every text element is measured with real font metrics before compositing and verified in the rendered pixels afterward; put publishing behind **one** publisher that only accepts human-approved rows; and add a **daily reconciliation** that alarms within 24 hours if anything is live on the channel without an approval record. Anchor/avatar reels stay parked until the HeyGen API deprecation (Oct 31, 2026) forces that separate decision.

Generation and uploads are currently paused/disabled. This plan keeps them paused until Phase 2 lands (§7).

### 1.3 Generation path (live until paused): Quick Pulse

`shotstack-reel.py --format pulse` (`run_quick_pulse`, line 8477):

1. Auto-picks a recent published article.
2. `generate_pulse_stats()` (line 903) asks **GPT-4o-mini at temperature 0.7** to invent the reel's entire on-screen text: hook stat, eyebrow/kicker, 2–3 stats, diaspora bullets, news summary, payoff. Results are **cached to disk** (`pulse-stats-{slug}.json`) and reused without revalidation.

## 2. Defect taxonomy

Each class: a real instance, the root cause, and why QA missed it.

**D1 — Text overflows the frame (edge cropping).**
Instances: Kiran's Oct 9 flag ("letters cropped at edges"); three fix commits in one day, Oct 10 (`2d3330c7` stretched images + edge clipping, `c4e50473` diaspora/what-happened beats, `b1e1cdd6` caption 42px→36px + `white-space:normal`); recurrence Oct 12 in the hero-stat beat of `dOdIzexK70M` — the caption "Indian-American Republicans on November ballot" rendered as one unwrapped full-width line.
Root cause: text is laid out by Shotstack's remote HTML renderer at fixed font sizes in full-width containers; **no component measures rendered text width against the frame before submitting**. Each fix narrowed one font or one beat.
Why QA missed it: Pulse runs no QA (§1.3). The anchor vision-QA samples 5 frames and scores subjectively; edge geometry is not in its checks.

**D2 — Wrong facts in on-screen text.**
Instance: the same Oct 12 Short's eyebrow read **"ELECTIONS 2023"** for a story about the 2026 ballot.
Root cause: every string in a Pulse reel is free-generated by GPT-4o-mini (temperature 0.7). The extraction prompt says "NEVER invent" — an instruction, not a check. The eyebrow field is unconstrained free text; no step verifies any number, year, or name in the scene text against the source article. Cached stats are reused unvalidated.
Why QA missed it: no fact-check step exists in any of the three QA implementations.

**D3 — Vendor watermark burned into a public video.**
Instance: Microsoft/Vance Short `vEqk-X9_qxY`, live with the Shotstack stage watermark.
Root cause: stage/sandbox renders are watermarked; both sandbox and production keys live in the same env file; which environment a render used is a CLI default, not an enforced, recorded invariant; the uploader (path 1) performs no content inspection at all.
Why QA missed it: no watermark check exists anywhere; the file reached the uploader by simply existing in the directory.

**D4 — Ungated auto-publishing.**
Instance: §1.6 — 8 Shorts in 4 days.
Root cause: the uploader's candidate set is *"any MP4 in this folder not in my private log."* Approval was a social convention ("rows stay pending until Kiran approves") that this code path never implemented.
Why QA missed it: not a QA failure — a governance failure. No component owned the rule "nothing publishes without approval."

**D5 — Fabricated QA state in the database.**
Instance: `qa_passed=True`, `qa_score=8` (the function's default argument) on all 12 October Pulse rows.
Root cause: `register_reel()` was written to satisfy downstream consumers that filter on `qa_passed`, so it asserts it.
Why it matters beyond honesty: `distribute-reels.py` trusts `qa_passed` as its **only** gate — D5 silently arms path 2 to publish everything, to four platforms.

**D6 — Reconciliation blindness.**
Instance: for 4 days, the database said 8 reels were "pending, never posted" while they accumulated views on YouTube.
Root cause: two partial ledgers (`prebuilt_reels`, `youtube-log.json`), zero comparison jobs. The system's health checks monitor articles and the website; nothing monitors the channel.

**D7 — Overlapping text layers.**
Instance: 2026-06-14 QA findings (`pipeline/reels/qa-findings-2026-06-14.md`): caption track and lower-third track occupied the same zone; vision QA scored a render **0** for it; root cause was independent tracks placing text without a shared layout model. Fixed then by repositioning; the *class* is identical in root to D1 and remains possible wherever tracks compose text independently (anchor captions, lower-thirds, stat cards).

**D8 — Stretched / distorted imagery.**
Instances: 2026-06-10 run log — anchor render failed QA for "stretched images"; Oct 9 — Kiran flagged an "elongated" reel image; Oct 10 fix commit `2d3330c7`.
Root cause: images fitted to 9:16 with non-uniform scaling in some paths. Pulse now pre-crops the hero (`_precrop_hero_9x16`) — an instance-level fix inside one builder, not a system-wide rule (cover-crop only, focal-point aware) enforced for every image in every format.

**D9 — Irrelevant B-roll.**
Instance: documented 2026-06-14 and still the recorded failure mode that day: same-category article images used as scene B-roll regardless of topic (a Capitol building in an NRI banking reel); QA scores of 4–5 with "image relevance is low." The findings doc's recommended fix (search by scene description first) was recorded as pending.
Root cause: image selection optimizes for *availability within the category pool*, not scene relevance; no relevance check with teeth (vision QA can complain, and the reel still ships after a re-roll or a threshold pass).

**D10 — Zero-visual reels.**
Instance: AGENTS.md records the June 25 guard — `shotstack-reel.py` aborts pre-render when zero scenes have a real visual, because all-bare-navy text-card reels failed QA ("OVERLAPPING TEXT"). Pulse is by design one static hero image behind ~35 s of cards — legal under the guard, monotonous in practice (§6).

**D11 — Render-credit exhaustion as a silent outage.**
Instance: 2026-06-10 run log — production credits at 0.19 against 0.50 required; the run produced 0 reels; discovery was by reading the log.
Root cause: a metered external dependency with no balance monitoring or alerting.

**D12 — Duplicate-upload risk across publishers.**
Instance: `reel-pipeline.py` line 2313 comment documents centralizing uploads after pipeline + distributor double-uploaded; path 1 then reintroduced an independent uploader keyed to a different ledger. Any two of paths 1–3 running in the same week can double-post the same story.

**Pattern across D1–D12:** every defect was previously *seen* — in QA findings docs, run logs, fix commits, or Kiran's own flags — and each was closed with a local patch. The system has never had (a) measurement before render, (b) verification of the rendered artifact, or (c) a single governed path to public. The strategy below is those three things.

## 3. Rendering architecture options

Evaluation criteria: layout determinism (can overflow be *impossible by construction*?), cost per reel at 2/day, build + maintenance effort, failure modes. Prices checked Oct 12, 2026 against Shotstack's published pricing page (shotstack.io/pricing): 1 credit = 1 rendered minute; pay-as-you-go $0.20/min; Starter $39/mo incl. 200 credits; overage billed at a premium on subscriptions. Internal logs put a production render at ~$0.20 / 0.5 credits for a ~35 s reel (June 2026 accounting) — consistent with per-minute pricing.

### Option A — Stay on Shotstack; add a strict design system + pre-render measurement

Keep the current engine and the in-flight urgent patch (a `fit_text` estimator with a hard `TextFitError`, now in the working tree). Add: template-level text budgets, post-render frame QA (§5), governance (§5.3–5.4).

- **Layout determinism: weakest of the three.** Shotstack renders HTML/CSS in *its* Chromium with *its* font stack. Any Python-side width estimate is an approximation of a renderer we don't control — the Oct 10→12 recurrence happened under exactly this regime (CSS wrapping behavior, e.g. `white-space`, differed from intent). Post-render pixel QA becomes the real guarantee; pre-render measurement only reduces waste.
- **Cost/reel:** ~$0.10–0.20 render + ~$0.01–0.03 vision QA. At 2/day ≈ **$8–15/mo** all-in. Negligible.
- **Effort:** lowest — days, building on existing code and the in-flight patch.
- **Failure modes:** renderer behavior drift (silent), credit exhaustion (D11), stage/production key confusion (D3), vendor outage, HTML/CSS edge cases per template. Kinetic typography already built — the one real asset here.

### Option B — Local deterministic rendering (PIL + ffmpeg)

Compose every scene locally: PIL renders text with the **actual font files** (`ImageFont.getlength` gives exact pixel widths — overflow is excluded by construction, and wrapping is computed, not hoped for); ffmpeg assembles scenes, music, fades/slides (xfade), and encodes. This is not greenfield: `generate-reel.py` already renders PIL text cards at 1080×1920, and `reel-pipeline.py` already assembles reels with ffmpeg. A Remotion (React/headless-Chromium) variant of this option exists — real CSS layout with pinned fonts — but adds a Node toolchain and a license whose current company terms we'd need to verify; PIL/ffmpeg avoids both and matches in-house skills.

- **Layout determinism: strongest.** The pixels QA inspects are produced by the same metrics that laid them out. Text-fit proof is exact, not estimated.
- **Cost/reel:** **$0 render fees.** A 35 s 1080×1920 encode is a few CPU-minutes on this VM (estimate; to be measured in Phase 1). Removes D11 entirely.
- **Effort:** highest of the three for Pulse — the 6-beat kinetic templates must be re-implemented (entrance animations become per-scene ffmpeg/PIL frame rendering). Realistic scope: one format (Pulse), a fixed template library (§4) — estimated 3–5 focused build days including the QA harness. Maintenance is ours, but the failure surface is code we can unit-test, including a golden-set regression suite (§7).
- **Failure modes:** VM CPU contention with the news pipeline (mitigate: nice/ionice, render at off-peak, queue of 1); VM environment drift — this box has a history of pip-package wipes (mitigate: pin deps, a startup self-check that fails closed); less flashy motion than Shotstack's HTML animation (mitigate: PIL can render per-frame animation — the cost is build time, not capability).

### Option C — Hybrid

Local layout & proofing (Option B's measurement + scene-card rendering to PNG), Shotstack only for motion/compositing of pre-rendered cards; or: Pulse local (Option B), anchor stays on Shotstack/HeyGen.

- **Determinism:** as strong as B for text (cards are finished pixels before Shotstack sees them); compositing can't reflow a PNG.
- **Cost/reel:** B's zero + A's ~$0.10–0.20 if Shotstack compositing is used.
- **Effort:** medium-high — most of B's build, plus keeping the Shotstack integration alive.
- **Failure modes:** inherits A's vendor/credit/watermark surface for whatever still renders there; two stacks to maintain. The anchor half of this hybrid is anyway blocked on the HeyGen v1/v2 API deprecation (**Oct 31, 2026** — see the standing reminder in the cron set), so "keep anchor as-is" has a hard expiry date regardless.

### Recommendation: **Option B for Pulse now; Option C's second half deferred as an explicit Phase 4 decision.**

Reasoning, plainly: every catastrophic defect class (D1, D2's rendering half, D3, D7, D8) is a *layout/rendering-trust* problem. Option A keeps the trust problem and papers it with better inspection. Option B removes it for the only format we currently ship, at zero marginal cost, using tools already proven in this repo — and Pulse (data cards + music over one image) is the easiest possible format to render locally with no creative loss. What B gives up (Shotstack's HTML kinetic type) is worth less than what it buys (a reel that *cannot* crop text). Shotstack is not deleted — it stays available, behind the same QA and governance, if the Phase 4 anchor decision wants it.

## 4. Design + content standard

One standard, enforced by code (budgets are build errors, not guidelines).

### 4.1 Canvas and safe areas (1080×1920)

Derived from published Shorts safe-zone guidance (vertical-video safe-zone template, drama-growth.chatvid.ai; Headliner safe-zone docs; HackMD Shorts template notes — sources §9), cross-checked against the Shorts player UI:

| Zone | Rule |
|---|---|
| Left/right text margin | **≥ 70 px** each side → max text width **940 px**. (Broadcast title-safe is ~5% ≈ 54 px; we take 70 px because our D1 failures happened *at* the edge) |
| Top exclusion | No text above **y = 140 px** (status bar, Shorts top gradient/search) |
| Bottom exclusion | No text below **y = 1580 px** (~340 px reserved: Shorts title/channel/CTA/caption block) |
| Right rail | Below y = 1200, keep critical glyphs left of **x = 940** (action rail ≈ 140 px) |
| Brand mark | Fixed position inside safe area; rendered from the same template code every time |

The current Pulse builder already keeps content in "the top 83%" vertically — the October failure was **horizontal**, where no rule existed. This standard closes the horizontal axis and tightens the vertical one.

### 4.2 Typography scale (Inter, the brand font; weights pinned as local files)

| Element | Size | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero stat | 120–160 px | ExtraBold | Numbers only; shrink-to-fit, never wrap |
| Hook line | 64–76 px | ExtraBold, ALL CAPS | ≤ 2 lines |
| Context/body text | 44–52 px | SemiBold | ≤ 3 lines |
| Stat label / diaspora bullet | 36–44 px | SemiBold | ≤ 2 lines |
| Eyebrow/kicker | 30–34 px | Bold, +6 px letter-spacing, ALL CAPS | 1 line, closed vocabulary (§4.4) |
| Brand line | 28–30 px | Bold | Fixed string, template-owned |

Minimum text size anywhere: **28 px**. Anything smaller is deleted, not shrunk.

### 4.3 Scene-template library with measured text budgets

Six templates, one per beat — no free-form scenes. Budgets below are **provisional ceilings**; Phase 1 replaces each with a number computed from real glyph metrics at the template's font/weight (worst-case string of that length must measure ≤ 940 px), and the builder enforces them as hard errors.

| Template | Elements (provisional budget) |
|---|---|
| `hook` | Hook line ≤ 2 lines × ~18 chars; no branding on frame 1 |
| `news` | Summary ≤ 3 lines × ~38 chars (≈ 25 words, matches current extraction rule) |
| `hero_stat` | Big ≤ 8 chars; sub ≤ 2 lines × ~34 chars; eyebrow ≤ 24 chars |
| `stat_grid` | 2–4 tiles; value ≤ 8 chars; label ≤ 2 lines × ~22 chars |
| `versus` | Labels ≤ 12 chars; values ≤ 2 lines × ~20 chars per side |
| `diaspora` | 2–3 bullets, each ≤ 2 lines × ~30 chars |
| `payoff` | Takeaway ≤ 2 lines × ~30 chars + fixed brand line |

A scene that exceeds budget **fails the build** with the offending string in the error — the fix is shorter copy (re-extract with the budget in the prompt), never a smaller font below the scale, never a wider box.

### 4.4 On-screen fact-check (new, non-optional)

Runs on the extracted scene JSON **before** render:

1. **Numeric grounding:** every number token in scene text (digits, %, $, years) must appear in the source article's headline/subheadline/body (normalized: commas, `$`, crore/lakh conversions handled explicitly). "ELECTIONS 2023" fails here: `2023` appears nowhere in a 2026-ballot article.
2. **Name grounding:** every capitalized multi-word entity in scene text must appear in the article text.
3. **Closed eyebrow vocabulary:** kickers come from a fixed list (BY THE NUMBERS, BREAKING, THE PRICE TAG, …) or are omitted. Free-invented kickers are how D2 happened.
4. **Extraction hygiene:** GPT extraction temperature → 0 for stats; **disk cache of stats is deleted** (or keyed by article-body hash and revalidated by checks 1–2 on every use).
5. Any failure → the reel is not built; the article is skipped and logged. No "warn and continue."

### 4.5 Image rules

- **Cover-crop only.** Non-uniform scaling is banned in code (one shared `fill_9x16()` helper; aspect preserved, crop, never stretch) — kills D8 as a class.
- Focal-point-aware crop using the existing `pipeline/focal_point.py` machinery from the article side.
- Full-bleed sources must be ≥ 1080 px wide; below that, the scene uses a branded background treatment instead of an upscaled blur.
- The article-side identity rule carries over unchanged: **named-person stories use verified photos only** — wrong photo is worse than no photo.
- Every scene must carry a real visual (the June 25 zero-visual abort becomes a build-time assertion for all formats) — kills D10's degenerate case; visual *variety* is addressed in §6.

## 5. QA architecture

Three layers, in order. The principle: **deterministic checks gate; opinions advise.**

### 5.1 Pre-render (build-time)

- Text measurement proof: every element measured with the real font; over-budget = build failure (§4.3).
- Fact-check gate (§4.4).
- Asset checks: image dimensions, focal point present, music file valid, total duration 25–40 s for Pulse.

### 5.2 Post-render deterministic visual QA (on the MP4 itself)

Runs on the rendered file, per scene (frames at scene midpoint + one after entrance animation settles — Pulse has 6 beats, so ≥ 6–12 frames, not 5 random samples):

1. **Edge-touch detection:** for each frame, scan the outer 40 px bands; high-contrast glyph-like pixels touching a band inside a text region = **fail**. (PIL/numpy only — deliberately no OpenCV dependency, given this VM's package-wipe history.)
2. **OCR verification:** OCR each scene frame (local tesseract — to be installed/pinned in Phase 1; cloud vision as fallback) and compare tokens against the approved scene script: every script token present, no unexpected numbers/years. This catches D2 **in the pixels**, including errors introduced after the JSON stage.
3. **Technical floor:** ffprobe (1080×1920, duration, streams), black-frame ratio, letterbox/content-fill — i.e., the useful parts of `reel_qa_gate.py`, kept.
4. **Watermark tripwire:** render-environment attestation — the reel record stores renderer + environment + key fingerprint; plus an OCR/template scan for vendor watermark text. Any stage/sandbox artifact = fail. (Belt and suspenders against D3; under Option B the risk mostly evaporates, the check stays for any Shotstack path.)
5. **Hard-fail semantics:** any failure → `status = qa_failed`, the row can never be approved or published, and there is **no override flag** in any cron-reachable code path. `qa_passed` may only be written by this QA module, with the actual per-check results stored in `qa_checks` — ending D5 by construction. A constant `qa_score` across rows becomes impossible; if it ever recurs, that's itself an alarm condition (§8).

The GPT-4o vision review survives only as an **advisory** quality score (hook strength, image relevance — useful signal for §6) attached to the approval view. It cannot pass or fail a reel.

### 5.3 The approval gate and the single publish path

- **State machine** in `prebuilt_reels`: `pending → qa_passed → approved → published` (+ `qa_failed`, `rejected`). Transitions are explicit; `approved` can only be set through Kiran's approval action — the same pattern as the article Shorts gate he already operates.
- **One publisher.** A single script publishes, and it reads **only** `status = approved` rows. For YouTube it uploads as **unlisted first**; the approval review *is* the real video on the real platform (this also surfaces mobile-player cropping exactly as viewers see it); approval flips it to public via the API. IG/X/Threads distribution, when re-enabled, goes through the same publisher and the same status check.
- **Mandatory write-back:** the publisher patches `yt_video_id` / `yt_posted_at` (and per-platform equivalents) in the same run; an upload whose write-back fails marks the run failed and alerts — the upload is treated as unaccounted until reconciled.
- **Retire the other paths:** `upload-youtube-shorts.py` deleted (its log archived); `distribute-reels.py` and `reel-pipeline.py`'s upload functions removed or reduced to calling the single publisher; `post-x-reel.py` archived. End state: `grep` for the YouTube upload endpoint in `pipeline/` returns exactly one file.

### 5.4 Reconciliation (the alarm that didn't exist)

Daily job (cheap, read-only): list the channel's uploads via the YouTube Data API (the existing OAuth already used for uploads), compare against `prebuilt_reels` rows with `status = published` + `yt_video_id`. **Any live video without a matching approved-and-published row → immediate alert to Kiran**, naming the video. Same check, weekly, for IG/X/Threads IDs. This bounds any future D4-class breach — from *any* cause, including manual uploads and tools not yet written — to < 24 hours of silent life. Known test set: run it today and it must flag exactly the 10 October uploads (8 daily-job + 2 manual) plus any other historical gaps it finds; those get backfilled as `published (legacy, pre-gate)` so the baseline is clean.

## 6. Quality uplift — making reels actually good

Reliability gets us to "not embarrassing." This section is about worth watching, grounded in what our own reels and data show today.

**What our own numbers say.** The upload script's comments encode hard-won channel data: voiceover variants averaged **3.9 views vs 80.4** for the music/data format; the two breakout hits (700+ views) were high-emotion, high-stakes stories; the watermarked Microsoft Short averaged ~1.1K views *despite* its defects — topic gravity (H-1B/green-card stakes for this audience) is currently doing more work than craft. Story selection is our strongest lever; the strategy protects it (fact-checked stats, diaspora-angle bullets are already in the format) and stops craft defects from taxing it.

**Format benchmarks (external, sourced — §9):**

- **Viewed-vs-swiped is decided in ~2 s.** A 5,000-Shorts analysis (Paddy Galloway's data, via dtptips) puts best performers at **70–90% "viewed"** and weak ones under 60%. Creator-observed promotion cutlines: **3-second retention ≥ 80%**, **average view percentage ≥ 70%**, 2-second swipe rate < 40% (social-flow growth playbook, summarizing 2025–26 public analyses — directional, not official). For 15–30 s Shorts, completion of 70–100% and first-3-second hold ≥ 70% are the commonly cited strong bands (Chiang Rai Times analytics guides). Our Pulse hook beat already puts the boldest claim first with no branding on frame 1 — keep that; measure it once Analytics pulls land (§8).
- **Brut India** (digital-first explainer publisher; its own channel framing is story-first explainers in new formats): bold burned-in text over real footage, one story per video, text legible with sound off. Lesson for us: our cards-over-one-static-image is visually poorer than text-over-relevant-footage/stills that *change with the beat*.
- **NDTV Micro** (launched Sept 2026; afaqs/MediaBrief coverage): built explicitly on "distillation, not shortening" — a complete story with context retained, in minutes. Lesson: our 6-beat arc (news → evidence → context → impact → payoff) is the right skeleton; the failure was never structure, it was execution trust. Don't redesign the arc; make every beat true and legible.
- **Anchor-led vertical explainers** (the Firstpost *Vantage* pattern): a trusted face delivers the story; retention rides on presenter credibility. Our anchor format exists but is parked behind HeyGen cost/quality questions and the Oct 31 API deprecation — Phase 4 decides it with data, not nostalgia.

**Concrete uplift commitments (in the Phase 1/3 scope, not aspirations):**

1. **One idea per reel** — enforced by the template library (one hook stat, one payoff). Multi-stat sprawl moves to stat_grid with ≤ 4 tiles.
2. **A visual change every beat** — each beat gets its own image/crop/treatment (focal-point crops of the hero + verified secondary images), ending the one-static-image-for-35 s monotony (D10's quality half).
3. **Captions/subtitles:** Pulse is music-only by design; the on-screen text *is* the caption track and must satisfy §4 budgets at reading speed — budget check includes a chars-per-second ceiling per beat.
4. **Hook discipline:** frame 1 carries the single most concrete number + named entity (already the extraction prompt's rule — now also *verified*, §4.4).
5. **Sound-off legibility, sound-on reward:** music bed retained; nothing essential is audio-only.
