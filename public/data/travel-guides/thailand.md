## Why Thailand?

Thailand is practically an honorary second home for Indians — the flight is under five hours from Delhi, the food has flavours your palate already understands, and Buddhist temples feel familiar in a way that European cathedrals never quite do. For NRIs living in America, Thailand offers the rare combination of genuine exoticism at a fraction of what you would spend on a European holiday. Bangkok's chaos mirrors Mumbai. Chiang Mai's cool mountains recall Darjeeling. And the southern islands — Phuket, Krabi, Koh Samui — deliver postcard beaches without the Maldives price tag. Whether you are planning a family reunion trip or a couples' getaway, Thailand consistently over-delivers.

## Top Experiences

**1. Grand Palace & Wat Pho, Bangkok** — The Grand Palace complex is Thailand's equivalent of the Taj Mahal — non-negotiable. Arrive at 8:30 AM when the gates open to beat tour groups. Wat Pho next door has the reclining Buddha and the best traditional Thai massage school in the country.

**2. Chiang Mai Night Bazaar & Temples** — The old city has over 300 temples within its moated walls. Doi Suthep at sunset is magnificent. The Sunday Walking Street market runs for a mile and sells everything from handmade silk to ₹200 pad thai.

**3. Phi Phi Islands Day Trip** — Take the speedboat from Phuket or Krabi. Maya Bay is open again after its environmental recovery. Snorkelling here rivals anything in Southeast Asia. Go early to avoid the midday crowds.

**4. Floating Markets, Bangkok** — Damnoen Saduak is the famous one, but Amphawa (open weekends) is more authentic and less touristy. Go in the morning, eat pad thai from a boat, and take the firefly tour at dusk.

**5. Elephant Nature Park, Chiang Mai** — Skip the riding camps. This ethical sanctuary lets you feed, bathe, and walk with rescued elephants. Book the full-day experience at least two weeks in advance — it sells out.

## Getting There

From the US, Bangkok has excellent connections via Tokyo (ANA/JAL), Seoul (Korean Air), or direct on EVA Air via Taipei. Air India and IndiGo fly nonstop from Delhi and Mumbai. Thai Airways has a nonstop from Delhi. Budget around $600–900 for round-trip from major US cities. Domestic flights within Thailand are absurdly cheap — Bangkok to Chiang Mai or Phuket on AirAsia or Nok Air costs $30–60 one way.

## Where to Stay

Bangkok: Sukhumvit Soi 11–23 area puts you near the BTS Skytrain, great restaurants, and nightlife. Marriott Marquis and Westin Grande are solid NRI picks with proper vegetarian options. Budget travellers should look at Silom or Khao San Road. Phuket: Patong Beach for energy, Kata Beach for families. Chiang Mai: the Old City or Nimmanhaemin Road for café culture. Throughout Thailand, five-star hotels run $80–150/night — a fraction of equivalent properties in Europe.

## Food & the Desi Connection

Thai food shares DNA with Indian food — lemongrass instead of ginger, fish sauce instead of salt, but the same love of chillies, coconut, and rice. Pad Thai, green curry, and mango sticky rice are universally loved. Vegetarians: say "jay" (เจ) — it means vegan in Thai and every street vendor understands it. Bangkok has several Indian restaurants in Phahurat (Little India) and Sukhumvit. Chiang Mai's Khao Soi — a Burmese-influenced curry noodle — tastes like something your grandmother might have invented.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders get visa-free entry for up to 60 days. US citizens and Green Card holders get 30 days visa-free. Currency is Thai Baht (THB) — withdraw from ATMs for the best rate but beware the 220 THB fee per transaction. Get a local SIM at the airport — AIS or TrueMove cost 300 THB with unlimited data for a week. Thailand is safe, but watch for common tourist scams: tuk-tuk drivers offering "free" city tours that end at gem shops, jet ski damage scams in Phuket, and "closed today" claims near temples. Dress modestly at temples — covered shoulders and knees required. November to February is peak season with perfect weather. Avoid March–May when Bangkok hits 40°C.
