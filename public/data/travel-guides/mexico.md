## Why Mexico?

Mexico is hiding in plain sight for Indian Americans. It is closer than India, cheaper than Europe, requires no visa if you hold a US visa or Green Card, and offers a cultural richness that rivals the subcontinent itself. Ancient civilisations, colonial architecture, a food culture recognised by UNESCO, and beaches that compete with the Caribbean — Mexico has everything except the Indian diaspora's attention. That is changing. Direct flights from major US cities make Mexico City a weekend trip, and the Yucatán Peninsula has become a serious alternative to Cancún's spring-break reputation. For NRIs based in the US, Mexico is the most underrated international destination within easy reach.

## Top Experiences

**1. Mexico City Historic Centre & Teotihuacán** — The Zócalo, one of the world's largest public squares, anchors a colonial centre built on Aztec ruins. The Templo Mayor excavation sits next to the cathedral. Then drive 50 kilometres to Teotihuacán — the Pyramid of the Sun is the third-largest pyramid on earth. Arrive at 8 AM for crowd-free photos from the Pyramid of the Moon.

**2. Oaxaca Food & Mezcal** — Mexico's culinary capital. Take a cooking class to learn mole negro (a sauce with 30+ ingredients), eat tlayudas at the Mercado 20 de Noviembre, and visit a mezcal distillery in the surrounding valleys. Indian palates appreciate Oaxacan food instinctively — the complexity and spice levels are comparable. The Day of the Dead celebrations here in late October are Mexico's most authentic.

**3. Chichén Itzá & Cenotes, Yucatán** — The great Maya pyramid of Kukulkán is one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Visit early morning to beat tour buses. Afterward, swim in a cenote — Ik Kil and Suytun are nearby. These limestone sinkholes, sacred to the Maya, offer surreal turquoise swimming in underground caverns.

**4. Tulum Beach Ruins** — The only major Maya ruin overlooking the Caribbean Sea. The clifftop temples against turquoise water is one of Mexico's most photographed scenes. Visit at opening (8 AM) then descend to the beach below for a swim. Stay in Tulum town rather than the overpriced hotel zone — better food, lower prices, more character.

**5. San Miguel de Allende** — A colonial hill town that Condé Nast Traveler readers have repeatedly voted the world's best city. Cobblestone streets, Baroque churches, rooftop bars, and a thriving expat art scene. The Parroquia de San Miguel Arcángel, a pink neo-Gothic church, dominates the skyline. Perfect for a two-night stay en route between Mexico City and Guanajuato.

## Getting There

Mexico City (MEX) has direct flights from most major US cities — 4.5 hours from New York, 3.5 from Houston, 4 from Los Angeles. Cancún (CUN) receives direct flights from across the US and seasonal charters from India (via Dubai). Aeromexico, United, American, and Delta all serve Mexico extensively. Within Mexico, VivaAerobus and Volaris are budget carriers connecting cities cheaply. For the Yucatán, fly into Cancún and drive — Tulum is 2 hours, Mérida is 3.5 hours. Mexico City to Oaxaca is a 1-hour flight or a scenic 6-hour drive.

## Where to Stay

Mexico City's Roma and Condesa neighbourhoods are the base for first-time visitors — tree-lined streets, Art Deco buildings, and walkable cafés and restaurants. Boutique hotels run $80–200 per night. In Oaxaca, Casa Oaxaca and Hotel Los Amantes offer colonial charm at $100–250. Tulum has luxury eco-hotels from $200–600, though budget cabañas still exist at $40–80. All-inclusive resorts in the Riviera Maya (Cancún to Tulum) range from $150 to $800 per night. Airbnb is excellent throughout Mexico and often 40% cheaper than hotels.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders with a valid US visa, Green Card, or Canadian visa can enter Mexico without a Mexican visa for up to 180 days. This makes Mexico the easiest international trip for US-based NRIs. The currency is the Mexican Peso (MXN) — roughly 17 per US dollar. US dollars are accepted in tourist zones but you will get a better rate paying in pesos. Street food is safe and extraordinary — follow the locals to busy stalls. Tap water is not potable; drink bottled or purified water. Mexico City's altitude (2,200 metres) can cause mild altitude effects — hydrate well on arrival. Uber works in all major cities and is safer than street taxis. Spanish is essential outside tourist areas — download Google Translate offline. Mexico is far safer than headlines suggest, but exercise normal urban caution in cities and avoid driving at night on rural highways.
