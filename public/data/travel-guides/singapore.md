## Why Singapore?

Singapore is the one international city where an Indian can land, eat, pray, and navigate entirely in familiar cultural codes. Little India is not a tourist novelty — it is a living, thriving neighbourhood. Tamil is one of the four official languages. Thaipusam is a national holiday. For NRIs in America introducing children to Asian culture without the sensory overload of India, Singapore is the perfect gateway — clean, safe, English-speaking, and dripping with Indian heritage beneath its futuristic skyline.

## Top Experiences

**1. Gardens by the Bay & Supertree Grove** — The Supertree light show at 7:45 PM and 8:45 PM is free and spectacular. The Cloud Forest dome and Flower Dome are worth the entry fee. Budget two hours minimum.

**2. Marina Bay Sands & Merlion Park** — The rooftop infinity pool is hotel-guests-only, but the SkyPark observation deck is open to all. Walk along the Helix Bridge to Merlion Park for the classic Singapore photo.

**3. Hawker Centers** — Singapore's hawker centres are UNESCO-recognised. Maxwell Food Centre (near Chinatown) for Hainanese chicken rice, Lau Pa Sat for satay street at night, and Tekka Centre in Little India for the best biryani, tosai, and fish head curry outside Chennai.

**4. Sentosa Island** — Universal Studios, Adventure Cove Waterpark, the S.E.A. Aquarium, and Siloso Beach packed into one island. Perfect for families with kids. Take the free Sentosa Express monorail from VivoCity mall.

**5. Little India & Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple** — Walk through the colourful streets of Serangoon Road. Visit the temple, eat at Komala Vilas or Banana Leaf Apolo (legendary fish head curry), and stock up on spices, sarees, and gold at Mustafa Centre — Singapore's 24-hour Indian department store.

## Getting There

Singapore Airlines flies nonstop from Newark, JFK, LAX, SFO, Seattle, and Houston. Flight time is 17–19 hours. United also has nonstop options. From India, Singapore is 4–5 hours from most cities with carriers like IndiGo, Air India, Scoot, and Singapore Airlines. Changi Airport — consistently ranked the world's best — makes arriving in Singapore a pleasure in itself. The Jewel complex has a 40-metre indoor waterfall.

## Where to Stay

Marina Bay: The Fullerton, Marina Bay Sands, Ritz-Carlton — all within walking distance of the major sights. Orchard Road: shopping district with mid-range options like Marriott Tang Plaza and Holiday Inn. Little India: Budget-friendly and culturally familiar — try Hotel 81 or Wanderlust. Clarke Quay: Nightlife-adjacent. For families, Sentosa has resort options. Singapore hotels are expensive by Asian standards — budget $120–250/night for mid-range, $300+ for luxury.

## Food & the Desi Connection

This is where Singapore shines for Indian travellers. The country has one of the largest Indian populations outside India, and the food reflects it. Tekka Centre in Little India serves authentic South Indian breakfast — idli, dosa, vada — from 6 AM. Banana Leaf Apolo has served legendary fish head curry since 1974. Komala Vilas is a pure vegetarian institution. Beyond Indian food, do not miss: Hainanese chicken rice, laksa, chilli crab (a must, budget $40–60 per crab), and kaya toast at Ya Kun Kaya Toast. Every hawker centre has vegetarian and halal options clearly marked.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders need a visa — apply through VFS. A valid US visa does NOT grant visa-free entry (a common misconception). US citizens get 90-day visa-free entry. The currency is Singapore Dollar (SGD) — roughly 1.35 to the USD. Singapore is the safest city in Asia — possibly the world. Public transport (MRT + buses) is exceptional and covers everything; skip taxis unless it is raining. Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber) is the ride-hailing app. Chewing gum is famously restricted. Fines for littering start at S$300. English is spoken everywhere. Singapore is hot and humid year-round — there is no "best season," just pack light clothes and an umbrella. Carry a light jacket for aggressively air-conditioned malls and MRT stations.
