## Why Iceland?

Iceland is the trip that makes you feel like you have left Earth. For NRIs who have spent years grinding through the American work cycle — visa renewals, performance reviews, 401(k) optimisations — Iceland offers a hard reset that no beach vacation can match. This is a country where you can stand between two continental plates, walk behind a waterfall, soak in a volcanic hot spring, and watch the northern lights dance overhead, all in a single day. The landscapes are so alien that NASA trained Apollo astronauts here. It is also one of the most accessible "bucket list" destinations from the US East Coast — closer to New York than Los Angeles is, with Icelandair offering cheap stopovers on transatlantic flights to Europe.

The Indian community in Iceland is tiny but growing, mostly professionals in tech and healthcare. The country is small enough that you can cover the highlights in 5–7 days, making it perfect for the kind of focused vacation NRIs prefer over open-ended backpacking. There are no dangerous animals, virtually no crime, the tap water tastes like bottled mineral water, and the air quality will make your lungs wonder what they have been breathing in New Jersey all these years.

## Top Experiences

**1. The Golden Circle** — This 300-kilometre loop from Reykjavik covers the three essentials: Thingvellir National Park (where the Eurasian and North American plates meet), the Geysir geothermal area (where Strokkur erupts every 5–8 minutes), and Gullfoss waterfall (a thundering two-tiered cascade). Self-drive in 6–8 hours, or book a guided tour for $70–90 per person.

**2. Blue Lagoon** — The milky-blue geothermal pool set in a black lava field is Iceland's most famous image. The Comfort package starts at $75 (includes entry, silica mud mask, and one drink). Book 2–3 weeks in advance — it sells out consistently. The newer Sky Lagoon near Reykjavik offers a similar experience with an infinity edge overlooking the ocean for about $55.

**3. South Coast Waterfalls & Black Sand Beach** — Drive the Ring Road south from Reykjavik to hit Seljalandsfoss (walk behind it), Skógafoss (climb the 527 steps beside it), and Reynisfjara black sand beach with its hexagonal basalt columns. This is a full day trip — 300 km round trip. Watch the sneaker waves at Reynisfjara; they have killed tourists. Do not turn your back on the ocean.

**4. Glacier Hiking on Sólheimajökull** — Walk on an actual glacier tongue with crampons and an ice axe. Guided tours run about $85–100 for 3 hours. No experience needed, but you must be reasonably fit. The blue ice crevasses and volcanic ash layers tell a visual story of Iceland's geological history.

**5. Northern Lights (September–March)** — Reykjavik's light pollution is manageable but driving 30 minutes outside the city dramatically improves visibility. Guided tours run $60–80 and include hot chocolate and blankets. The solar maximum in 2025–2026 means aurora activity is at peak intensity — this is the best window in a decade.

**6. Whale Watching from Húsavík** — Iceland's whale-watching capital has a 97–99% sighting rate in summer. North Sailing operates carbon-neutral oak boats for about $85. Humpback whales are the stars, but blue whales are occasionally spotted. The Whale Museum in town is small but excellent ($15).

**7. Reykjavik Culture Walk** — Start at Hallgrímskirkja church (take the elevator to the top for $12), walk down Laugavegur shopping street, visit the Harpa concert hall (free to walk in — the glass facade is extraordinary), and end with a lobster soup at Sægreifinn (the Sea Baron) for about $25.

## Getting There

Icelandair flies nonstop from New York (JFK), Boston, Newark, Chicago, Washington DC, Minneapolis, Denver, Seattle, and Portland to Reykjavik Keflavik (KEF). Flight time from the East Coast is just 5–5.5 hours — shorter than flying to California. Round-trip fares run $350–700 depending on season. Icelandair's free Stopover program lets you pause in Iceland for up to 7 days at no extra airfare cost on flights to Europe — one of the best travel hacks available. PLAY, Iceland's budget airline, also operates from Baltimore, Boston, and New York with fares as low as $200 one-way. Keflavik Airport is 50 minutes from Reykjavik by shuttle bus ($25 one-way via Flybus) or rental car.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** The Retreat at Blue Lagoon is Iceland's most exclusive hotel — built into a lava field with private lagoon access ($1,000–2,500/night). In Reykjavik, the Reykjavik EDITION (Marriott) is the city's design flagship ($350–600/night). Ion Adventure Hotel in Thingvellir offers Northern Lights views from an in-room hot tub ($300–500/night).

**Mid-range:** Fosshotel Reykjavik is a reliable 4-star with clean Scandinavian design and excellent breakfast ($180–260/night). Tower Suites Reykjavik offers apartment-style suites with kitchenettes ($200–350/night). Outside Reykjavik, Hotel Rangá in South Iceland is a highland lodge with an observatory for northern lights ($200–350/night).

**Budget:** CityHub Reykjavik offers compact "smart rooms" at hostel prices ($80–100/night). KEX Hostel is a converted biscuit factory with a great bar and communal vibe ($35–60/night for private rooms). Guesthouses along the Ring Road run $80–140/night and often include breakfast.

## NRI Tips

US citizens, Green Card holders, and OCI cardholders with US passports enter Iceland visa-free for up to 90 days (Schengen zone). Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa — apply at the VFS Global Iceland centre. Currency is the Icelandic króna (ISK), but cards are accepted absolutely everywhere — even remote gas stations and food trucks. You genuinely do not need cash. Iceland is eye-wateringly expensive — budget $200–250/day per person for food, fuel, and activities. A basic restaurant meal is $25–35, a beer is $10–12. Renting a car is essential outside Reykjavik — compact cars run $60–80/day, 4x4s needed for highland roads are $120–180/day. The F-roads (highland interior) require a 4x4 and are only open June–September. The weather changes every 20 minutes — pack waterproof layers, a wind-resistant jacket, and good hiking boots even in summer. Temperatures rarely exceed 15°C in summer. Tap water is excellent — do not buy bottled water. Tipping is not expected. The hot water smells like sulphur (it is geothermal) — this is normal and safe. Download the Veður app for hyper-local weather and the Aurora Forecast app for northern lights predictions.
