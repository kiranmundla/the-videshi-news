## Why Sri Lanka?

Sri Lanka is India's neighbour that most of the diaspora has never visited — and that is a mistake. It packs an astonishing density of experiences into an island smaller than Tasmania: ancient Buddhist ruins, colonial hill stations, world-class surf, leopard safaris, and a cuisine that will make you rethink everything you thought you knew about South Asian food. For NRIs, Sri Lanka offers cultural familiarity with enough foreignness to feel like a genuine adventure. The Tamil connection in the north and east adds a personal dimension for many diaspora families. And at roughly half the cost of Southeast Asia, it remains extraordinarily good value.

## Top Experiences

**1. Sigiriya Rock Fortress** — A fifth-century palace built atop a 200-metre volcanic plug, surrounded by landscaped gardens and frescoed caves. The climb is steep but manageable. Arrive at 7 AM opening to avoid both crowds and heat. The views from the summit across the central plain are staggering.

**2. Train from Kandy to Ella** — Routinely listed among the world's most scenic rail journeys. Seven hours through tea plantations, waterfalls, and cloud forest. Book first class (₹800) for reserved seats, or join the locals in unreserved carriages and hang from the open doors for photos. The 9 Arch Bridge near Ella is the money shot.

**3. Galle Fort** — A walled Dutch colonial fort on the southwest coast, now filled with boutique hotels, cafés, and galleries. Walk the ramparts at sunset. The literary festival in January draws writers from across Asia. Galle is also the base for whale watching — blue whales pass within miles of shore from November to April.

**4. Yala National Park Safari** — The highest density of leopards anywhere on earth. Morning jeep safaris offer excellent chances of sighting. Yala also has elephants, sloth bears, and crocodiles. Book a private jeep rather than joining a convoy — the experience is vastly better.

**5. Jaffna & the North** — Still undervisited since the civil war ended in 2009, Jaffna offers Tamil Hindu culture, Nallur Kandaswamy Kovil, and extraordinary seafood — the Jaffna crab curry is legendary. For Tamil NRIs especially, this is a deeply moving visit. The peninsula's palmyra-lined roads and Dutch-era churches add colonial layers.

## Getting There

Colombo's Bandaranaike International Airport receives flights from Chennai (1 hour), Delhi (3.5 hours), and Mumbai (2.5 hours). SriLankan Airlines and IndiGo operate multiple daily services. From the US, connect through Delhi, Dubai, or Singapore. Within Sri Lanka, distances are short but roads can be slow — a private car with driver costs about $40–60 per day and is the best way to travel. Trains are scenic but schedules are irregular.

## Where to Stay

Sri Lanka's hotel scene punches above its weight. Aman and Resplendent Ceylon (Cape Weligama, Ceylon Tea Trails) set the luxury standard at $400–1,000 per night. Boutique guesthouses in Galle Fort, Ella, and Trincomalee offer exceptional charm at $60–150. Budget travellers can find clean guesthouses island-wide from $20 per night. Homestays, particularly in the hill country, provide the warmest hospitality and the best food. Book directly for better rates.

## NRI Tips

Indian citizens need an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) — apply online at least 48 hours before travel. OCI holders follow the same process. Sri Lankan rupees are the currency; carry cash for rural areas, though cards work in cities and tourist zones. A local SIM from Dialog costs about $5 at the airport with generous data. Sri Lanka drives on the left. The country is predominantly Buddhist — dress modestly at temples (cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes). Political sensitivities around the civil war remain real, especially in the north — be respectful and listen more than you speak. Dengue risk exists year-round; use mosquito repellent diligently.
