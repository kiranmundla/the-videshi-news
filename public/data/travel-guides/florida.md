## Why Florida?

Florida is practically the unofficial NRI vacation state. Between the massive Indian communities in Tampa, Orlando, and South Florida, the Hindu temples dotting every major metro, and the sheer density of vegetarian-friendly restaurants, Florida feels like a home away from home — except with beaches, theme parks, and year-round sunshine. For diaspora families with kids, the Orlando corridor alone justifies the trip: Walt Disney World, Universal Studios, and Kennedy Space Center in one stretch. But Florida is far more than theme parks. Miami's Art Deco district, the Everglades, the Florida Keys, and the Gulf Coast's sugar-white beaches make this one of the most versatile family destinations in America.

For NRIs scattered across the colder states — New Jersey, Illinois, Michigan — Florida is the domestic escape that requires no passport, no visa headache, and no jet lag. Direct flights run from every major US city, and a week here costs a fraction of an international trip. Desi groceries and restaurants are everywhere. Tampa alone has more than 20 Indian restaurants, and the BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir in Bartow is one of the largest Hindu temples in the Western Hemisphere.

## Top Experiences

**1. Walt Disney World, Orlando** — The gold standard of family vacations. With four theme parks, two water parks, and Disney Springs, you need a minimum of four days to cover the highlights. Book Lightning Lane passes ($15–30/ride) for headliners like Avatar Flight of Passage and Tron Lightcycle Run. Indian families: the park has surprisingly decent vegetarian options — try Satu'li Canteen in Animal Kingdom. Go during January or early February to avoid the worst crowds.

**2. Kennedy Space Center, Cape Canaveral** — An absolute must for families with curious kids. The Space Shuttle Atlantis exhibit is jaw-dropping, and the bus tour to Launch Complex 39 where Apollo missions launched gives you chills. General admission is $75 for adults, $65 for kids. Add the astronaut lunch experience for $30 if your children love space. Arrive when gates open at 10 AM to cover everything in one day.

**3. The Florida Keys & Key West** — Drive the Overseas Highway from Miami to Key West, 113 miles of bridges and islands over turquoise water. It is one of America's most scenic drives. Stop at Bahia Honda State Park for the best beach in the Keys, snorkel at John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park (the only underwater park in the US), and end in Key West for sunset at Mallory Square. The whole drive takes 3.5 hours without stops.

**4. Everglades Airboat Tour** — An airboat ride through the River of Grass is unlike anything else in the US. You will see alligators, herons, and cypress swamps from just feet away. Book with Everglades Holiday Park or Coopertown Airboats ($28–35 per person, 40 minutes). Go in the dry season (December–April) when wildlife concentrates near water channels.

**5. Miami Beach & South Beach** — Walk the Art Deco Historic District on Ocean Drive, swim at South Beach, and explore Wynwood Walls for street art. For Indian families, head to Deccan Spice or Ghee Indian Kitchen in Miami proper. The nightlife is world-class, but the daytime beach culture is equally spectacular. Park at public garages ($20/day) rather than fighting for street spots.

**6. Clearwater Beach & Gulf Coast** — Consistently ranked among America's best beaches, Clearwater's calm, warm Gulf waters are perfect for families with young children. Pier 60 at sunset has free entertainment and craft vendors. Combine with a visit to the Clearwater Marine Aquarium (home of Winter the dolphin, $28/adult). Stay in the Clearwater-St. Pete area for a quieter, more affordable alternative to Miami.

**7. BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir, Bartow** — One of the largest Hindu temples in the Western world, built with Italian Carrara marble and Turkish limestone, entirely by volunteer craftsmen. The campus includes exhibition halls, gardens, and a vegetarian cafeteria. Free entry, open daily. It is a stunning piece of architecture and a deeply moving experience for any diaspora Indian visiting Florida.

## Getting There

Florida is one of the easiest states to reach from anywhere in the US. Orlando International (MCO) and Miami International (MIA) are major hubs with nonstop flights from every large US city. Newark to Orlando is 2.5 hours, Chicago to Miami is 3 hours, and even the West Coast is only 5 hours nonstop. Fort Lauderdale (FLL) is a budget airline hub — Spirit and Frontier often have fares under $100 round trip. Tampa (TPA) serves the Gulf Coast. A rental car is essential — Florida is spread out, and public transit is limited outside downtown Miami. Interstate 4 connects Tampa to Orlando; the Florida Turnpike runs from Orlando to Miami.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** The Breakers in Palm Beach ($600–1,200/night) is old-money grandeur. In Miami, the Faena Hotel ($500–900) on Mid-Beach combines art, dining, and beach in one property. For Orlando, Four Seasons at Walt Disney World Resort ($700–1,500) is the best on-property luxury.

**Mid-Range:** Hyatt Regency Orlando ($180–280) puts you minutes from the parks. In Miami Beach, The Confidante ($200–350) is a stylish South Beach option. Clearwater's Hyatt Regency ($200–300) has Gulf-front rooms.

**Budget:** In Orlando, the Drury Inn near Disney ($120–170) includes free hot breakfast and evening cocktails. In Miami, Generator Hotel ($90–150) in Mid-Beach offers hostel-chic at budget prices. For the Keys, budget motels in Marathon run $120–180 and put you halfway down the island chain.

## NRI Tips

No visa or passport needed — Florida is domestic travel for US residents. That said, carry a valid photo ID (driver's license or passport) for park entry and domestic flights. Florida has no state income tax, which makes shopping cheaper — many NRIs plan electronics and luxury purchases here. The weather is subtropical: summer (June–September) brings daily afternoon thunderstorms and extreme humidity. Winter (December–March) is ideal, with temperatures in the 70s°F and low humidity. Hurricane season runs June through November — buy travel insurance if visiting during those months. Indian grocery stores (Patel Brothers, Sabzi Mandi) exist in every major Florida metro. Mosquito repellent is essential near the Everglades and Keys. Sunscreen is non-negotiable year-round — UV levels here are significantly higher than the Northeast.
