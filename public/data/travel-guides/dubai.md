## Why Dubai?

Dubai is where India's diaspora goes to feel rich, even on a budget. Nearly a third of Dubai's population is Indian, which means you will find Alphonso mangoes in the hypermarket, Malayalam on the metro announcements, and dosa at 2 AM. For NRIs in America, Dubai serves as the ultimate layover-turned-vacation — break up your India trip with three days of air-conditioned excess. The city is engineered for spectacle: the tallest building, the biggest mall, the most audacious artificial island. It is Las Vegas with better food, more safety, and no gambling.

## Top Experiences

**1. Burj Khalifa, Downtown Dubai** — Book the "At the Top" ticket for the 124th floor, or splurge on the 148th floor SKY lounge. Go at sunset for the best photos. The Dubai Fountain show below runs every 30 minutes after 6 PM and never gets old.

**2. Old Dubai: Gold Souk & Spice Souk** — Cross Dubai Creek on a traditional abra boat for one dirham and step into the oldest part of the city. The Gold Souk is the world's largest — prices are competitive and bargaining is expected. The Spice Souk next door smells like your grandmother's kitchen.

**3. Desert Safari** — The quintessential Dubai experience. A 4x4 dune-bashing ride followed by a BBQ dinner under the stars with belly dancing, henna, and shisha. Book through your hotel or a reputable operator — expect 200–350 AED per person.

**4. Dubai Marina & JBR Walk** — The Marina skyline at night is Dubai at its most photogenic. JBR (Jumeirah Beach Residence) has a public beach, waterfront dining, and Ain Dubai — the world's largest observation wheel.

**5. Global Village** — Open October through April, this massive outdoor theme park has pavilions from 90+ countries. The India Pavilion is always the loudest and most fun. Street food from every continent for under 20 AED.

## Getting There

Emirates flies nonstop from most major US cities — JFK, LAX, SFO, IAD, Chicago, Dallas, Houston, Seattle, Boston. Flight time is 12–14 hours. If you are breaking an India trip, Emirates' free Dubai stopover program gives you a visa and sometimes a hotel night for transit passengers. From India, flights from any major city take 3–4 hours and cost as little as ₹8,000 one way.

## Where to Stay

Downtown (near Burj Khalifa/Mall): Armani Hotel, Address Downtown for luxury. Rove Downtown for smart budget. Marina/JBR: great for beach access and nightlife. Deira: the old-school choice, close to the souks, significantly cheaper. Indian families love the all-inclusive resorts on Palm Jumeirah. Atlantis The Royal and Atlantis The Palm both have excellent vegetarian options. Budget tip: Airbnbs in Marina or JLT run $60–100/night with full kitchens.

## Food & the Desi Connection

Dubai has arguably the best Indian food outside India. Ravi Restaurant in Satwa has served legendary Pakistani-Punjabi food since 1978 — butter chicken and naan for 25 AED. Bombay Chowpatty, Calicut Paragon, and Asha's by Asha Bhosle cover every regional craving. For fine dining, try Tresind Studio — India's most innovative cuisine, reimagined. Vegetarians and Jains will have zero issues — menus everywhere cater to Indian dietary needs. Karachi Darbar, open 24 hours, is the NRI late-night institution.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders get a 14-day visa on arrival or can apply for a 30-day e-visa. US citizens get 30 days visa-free. US Green Card holders still need a visa. Currency is AED (dirham) — pegged to the dollar at roughly 3.67. Dubai is extremely safe — petty crime is nearly nonexistent. Dress is relaxed in tourist areas but modest in malls and public spaces. Alcohol is available at licensed restaurants and hotels. The metro is spotless, cheap, and covers most tourist areas. Friday is the weekend day — many offices close Thursday afternoon. The best months are November through March when temperatures are 20–30°C. Summer hits 48°C and the city moves entirely indoors.
