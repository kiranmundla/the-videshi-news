## Why Alaska?

Alaska is the trip that resets your sense of scale. For NRIs who have spent years in the predictable rhythms of suburban America — the commute, the Costco run, the school pickup — Alaska delivers a visceral reminder that the natural world is still staggeringly wild. Glaciers the size of cities calving into turquoise fjords, grizzly bears fishing for salmon in chest-deep rivers, the midnight sun refusing to set at 11 PM in June. There is nothing like it in the lower 48, nothing like it in India, nothing like it anywhere you have been. For the diaspora family that has checked off Europe, done the India trip, and exhausted the Caribbean, Alaska is the American adventure that feels genuinely foreign.

The state also has a practical appeal: it is domestic travel. No passport hassles, no visa questions, no currency conversion. Direct flights from Seattle take just 3.5 hours. A 7-day Alaska cruise — the most popular way to experience the state — departs from Seattle or Vancouver and handles all the logistics for you. For vegetarian NRI families, cruises are ideal because ships offer extensive meat-free dining. And the scenery from a ship's deck is unlike any other cruise destination on earth.

## Top Experiences

**1. Glacier Bay National Park** — Accessible primarily by cruise ship or small charter boat, Glacier Bay is the crown jewel of Alaska tourism. Watch massive tidewater glaciers calve icebergs into the sea from the deck of your ship. The Margerie Glacier alone is 21 stories tall and a mile wide. If you are on a cruise, your ship typically spends an entire day here. Independent visitors can fly to Gustavus and kayak the bay — an unforgettable multi-day adventure.

**2. Denali National Park** — Home to Denali (Mount McKinley), the tallest peak in North America at 20,310 feet. The park road extends 92 miles into wilderness, but private vehicles can only drive the first 15 miles. Take the Tundra Wilderness Tour bus ($115/adult, 6–8 hours) deep into the park for grizzly bear and caribou sightings. On clear days — roughly 30% of summer — Denali's snow-capped summit is visible from 70 miles away. Book lodging in the tiny village of Healy, 10 miles north.

**3. Kenai Fjords National Park, Seward** — Take the Major Marine Tours 6-hour cruise ($200/adult, $130/child) from Seward to see humpback whales, orcas, sea otters, puffins, and the Exit Glacier. The wildlife is almost guaranteed in summer. Seward is a 2.5-hour drive from Anchorage on the stunning Seward Highway, one of America's most scenic roads.

**4. Mendenhall Glacier, Juneau** — One of the most accessible glaciers in Alaska, just 12 miles from downtown Juneau. Hike the moderate Trail of Time (2.4 miles round trip) to the glacier viewing area. The Nugget Falls trail takes you to a massive waterfall fed by glacier melt. The glacier has been retreating dramatically — see it while you can. Free entry; parking is $5.

**5. Inside Passage Cruise** — A 7-day cruise through Alaska's Inside Passage — typically Seattle/Vancouver to Juneau, Skagway, Ketchikan, and Glacier Bay — is the most popular way to see Alaska. Holland America, Princess, and Norwegian run weekly departures May through September. Inside cabins start at $700/person; balcony cabins run $1,200–2,000. The scenery from a balcony cabin is worth every dollar.

**6. Northern Lights (Aurora Borealis)** — Visible from Fairbanks between September and March, the northern lights are a bucket-list experience. Chena Hot Springs Resort ($275/night) offers aurora viewing packages and natural hot springs. The combination of soaking in geothermally heated water while green curtains of light dance overhead is surreal.

**7. Alaska Railroad** — The Denali Star train from Anchorage to Fairbanks ($250–350 one-way, GoldStar dome car) passes through 12 hours of wilderness, crossing trestles over glacial rivers with Denali views. The Coastal Classic from Anchorage to Seward ($125, 4 hours) hugs Turnagain Arm with mountain-and-ocean panoramas.

## Getting There

Alaska Airlines operates nonstop flights from Seattle to Anchorage (3.5 hours), Juneau (2.5 hours), and Fairbanks (3.5 hours). From other US cities, connect in Seattle — direct flights from San Francisco, Los Angeles, Chicago, and Newark reach Seattle in 3–6 hours. Anchorage is the main gateway for road trips and Denali. Juneau is the capital but has no road connection to the rest of Alaska — you fly or cruise in. For cruises, most depart from Seattle (Pier 91) or Vancouver. The Alaska Highway from the lower 48 through Canada is a legendary 2,200-mile road trip, but requires 4–5 days of driving.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** Sheldon Chalet ($2,400/night, all-inclusive) sits at 6,000 feet on a glacial nunatak in Denali National Park — reachable only by helicopter. It is one of the most exclusive lodges on earth. In Juneau, the Juneau Hotel ($250–400) offers downtown waterfront luxury. The Alyeska Resort in Girdwood ($300–500) combines ski-resort luxury with glacier views.

**Mid-Range:** Talkeetna Alaskan Lodge ($200–350) has the best Denali views of any hotel, 2.5 hours from Anchorage. In Seward, the Harbor 360 Hotel ($180–280) overlooks Resurrection Bay. Anchorage's Hotel Captain Cook ($170–250) is the city's landmark downtown hotel.

**Budget:** Anchorage has solid chain hotels (SpringHill Suites, $130–180). In Denali, cabins at McKinley Creekside ($110–170) put you near the park entrance. Hostels exist in Juneau and Anchorage ($40–70/night) but book months ahead for summer.

## NRI Tips

Alaska is a US state — no passport needed for domestic travel. However, if your cruise departs from Vancouver, you will need a valid passport for the Canadian border crossing. Pack layers: summer temperatures range from 45°F to 65°F, with occasional 70°F days. Rain gear is essential — Juneau gets 60 inches of rain annually. Mosquitoes in interior Alaska (Denali, Fairbanks) are legendary in June and July — bring DEET-based repellent and head nets. Vegetarian options are limited outside Anchorage; cruises are far better for vegetarian dining. Cell phone service is spotty to nonexistent in most of Alaska outside cities — download offline maps. Daylight is extreme: Fairbanks gets 22 hours of daylight in June, which can disrupt sleep — bring an eye mask. Wildlife safety is serious — carry bear spray on any backcountry hike, and never approach bears or moose. Book summer activities (Denali buses, glacier cruises) at least 2–3 months in advance; popular dates sell out by March.
