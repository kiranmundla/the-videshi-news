## Why Dominican Republic?

The Dominican Republic is the Caribbean destination NRIs stumble upon when someone in the group chat says "I want beach, I want all-inclusive, and I want it cheap." And then they arrive and realize they've underestimated the place entirely. The DR — and Punta Cana in particular — has spent the last two decades building a resort infrastructure that rivals anything in Mexico or Jamaica, at prices that consistently run 20–30% lower. A five-star all-inclusive week in Punta Cana costs what a long weekend in Cabo costs. For NRI families running the math on a vacation that accommodates kids, aging parents, and a stretched post-tax budget, the DR is the answer that makes the spreadsheet work.

But there is more here than the resorts. Santo Domingo, the capital, is the oldest continuously inhabited European settlement in the Americas — its Zona Colonial is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with cobblestone streets, 16th-century cathedrals, and a history that predates the Pilgrims by over a century. The Samaná Peninsula offers whale watching that rivals anything in Baja California. The interior has mountain villages, waterfalls, and a Dominican culture — merengue, bachata, mangú for breakfast, dominos on every corner — that is vibrant, generous, and entirely its own. The DR is not just a beach trip. It is a beach trip that happens to sit on top of a genuinely fascinating country, if you choose to look.

## Top Experiences

**1. Punta Cana Beach Day** — Bávaro Beach and Playa Macao are the headliners, consistently ranked among the Caribbean's finest. The water is shallow, warm, and that specific shade of turquoise that makes your phone camera inadequate. Most all-inclusive resorts sit directly on Bávaro Beach, so your beach day is literally walking out the back door. If you want to escape the resort strip, Playa Macao (about 20 minutes north) is less developed, with powerful waves for boogie boarding and local food shacks serving fried fish and tostones for $5–8. Playa Juanillo (at Cap Cana) is postcard-perfect and less crowded.

**2. Zona Colonial, Santo Domingo** — A day trip worth every minute of the 2.5-hour drive from Punta Cana. The oldest cathedral in the Americas (Catedral Primada de América, 1512), the Alcázar de Colón (Columbus's son's palace), and the Fortaleza Ozama are all within walking distance. The Calle Las Damas is the oldest paved street in the Western Hemisphere. Guided walking tours run $30–50 per person and bring the history to life. Stop at El Mesón de la Cava — a restaurant inside a natural cave — for dinner ($25–40 per person). Many resorts offer organized Santo Domingo day trips for $80–120 per person including transport and lunch.

**3. Samaná Whale Watching (January–March)** — Every winter, thousands of humpback whales migrate to the Bay of Samaná to breed and calve. This is one of the most reliable whale-watching experiences in the world. Boat tours from the town of Samaná run $50–80 per person. The bay is calm, the sightings are virtually guaranteed during peak season (February), and the mothers with newborn calves are extraordinarily moving. Samaná is about 3–3.5 hours from Punta Cana — consider spending a night at the Gran Bahía Príncipe El Portillo ($150–200/night) to break up the drive.

**4. Scape Park at Cap Cana** — An adventure park about 15 minutes from most Punta Cana resorts featuring cenote-like freshwater lagoons (the Hoyo Azul is a stunning natural sinkhole), ziplines over the jungle, cultural experiences, and cave swimming. Full-day passes run $100–130 for adults, $50–65 for kids. The Hoyo Azul alone — a limestone sinkhole with electric-blue water at the base of a 75-foot cliff — is worth the visit. Less crowded and more natural than the Xcaret-style parks in Mexico. Book online for discounts.

**5. Catamaran Snorkeling Tour** — Multiple operators run half-day catamaran trips from Punta Cana to the natural pools of the Caribbean — shallow, calm areas where you can stand waist-deep in the ocean, drink rum punch, and snorkel among starfish and tropical fish. Tours run $60–90 per person and include an open bar, music, and usually a stop at a private beach. Marinarium and Saona Dreams are reliable operators. The vibe is festive and social — great for groups and couples. Saona Island full-day trips ($80–120) include a catamaran ride to a pristine island with lunch — the quintessential DR experience.

**6. Cigar and Rum Tour** — The Dominican Republic produces some of the world's finest cigars and rum. La Aurora Cigar Factory in Santiago (3 hours from Punta Cana) and Tabacalera de García in La Romana (1 hour from Punta Cana) offer factory tours ($15–25) where you can watch master rollers at work and buy directly at factory prices. For rum, the Ron Barceló factory in San Pedro de Macorís ($10–15 tour) and Brugal in Puerto Plata are both excellent. If you can't leave the resort area, most all-inclusive resorts have cigar bars with Dominican selections, and rum is served endlessly at the all-inclusive bars.

**7. Indigenous Eyes Ecological Park** — A 1,500-acre private reserve within the Puntacana Resort & Club featuring 12 freshwater lagoons, nature trails, a petting zoo, and dense tropical forest. Entry is $25 for non-resort guests. The lagoons are swimmable and crystal-clear — a peaceful alternative to the crowded resort pools. The park is also home to iguanas, hutias (large Caribbean rodents), and over 100 bird species. Perfect for a morning walk before the afternoon beach session.

## Getting There

Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ) is one of the busiest in the Caribbean, with nonstop service from many major US cities. From New York JFK, JetBlue and Delta fly direct in about 3.5 hours. From Miami, American Airlines offers multiple daily nonstops at just 2.5 hours. From Atlanta, Delta has daily nonstops in about 3.5 hours. From Boston, JetBlue flies direct in about 4 hours. Newark, Philadelphia, Charlotte, Fort Lauderdale, and Orlando also have nonstop options. Round-trip fares typically range from $300–550, with sales frequently dipping to $250 or below — the DR is one of the most affordable Caribbean flights from the East Coast.

Alternatively, Las Américas International Airport (SDQ) in Santo Domingo serves travelers heading to the capital or the south coast. La Romana International Airport (LRM) is closer to Casa de Campo and the southeastern resorts.

From Punta Cana airport, most all-inclusive resorts include complimentary airport transfers — confirm at booking. If not, private shuttles run $25–40 per person to the Bávaro resort strip (about 20–30 minutes). Uber does not operate reliably in the DR; use hotel transport or prearranged taxis. Renting a car is possible but unnecessary if you're resort-focused. For day trips to Santo Domingo or Samaná, book through your resort or a reputable tour company — driving conditions in the DR require alertness.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** Hard Rock Hotel & Casino Punta Cana ($450–600/night all-inclusive) is a massive, buzzing resort with 13 pools, a casino, a Tom Fazio golf course, and a Jack Nicklaus-designed course — the resort credits included in the rate can be used at the spa, a genuine perk. Excellence El Carmen ($350–500/night all-inclusive, adults only) is upscale and elegant with swim-up suites and outstanding restaurants. Casa de Campo Resort & Villas in La Romana ($220–400/night, not all-inclusive) is the country's most exclusive property — Teeth of the Dog golf course, Altos de Chavón artists' village, and a private marina.

**Mid-Range:** Lopesan Costa Bávaro ($350–475/night all-inclusive) is a newer property with exceptional design and a huge water park — excellent for families. Grand Palladium Punta Cana ($225–300/night all-inclusive) is a reliable choice with a massive property, five pools, and a good beach. Majestic Elegance Punta Cana ($250–350/night all-inclusive) has a gorgeous lobby, adults-only section, and consistently excellent food. JOIA Bávaro by Iberostar ($300–400/night all-inclusive, adults only) is a newer, design-forward option with superb reviews.

**Budget:** Wyndham Alltra Punta Cana ($200–290/night all-inclusive) offers solid value with a water park, seven restaurants, and direct beach access. Serenade Punta Cana Beach & Spa ($200–280/night all-inclusive) is a strong budget pick with a family-friendly vibe and private beach. Grand Palladium Bávaro Suites ($200–280/night all-inclusive) is the more affordable wing of the Grand Palladium complex. For non-all-inclusive budget stays, the town of Bávaro has guesthouses and small hotels from $50–80/night, though you'll need to taxi to the beach.

## NRI Tips

**Visa:** US citizens do not need a visa — just a valid passport and a Tourist Card ($10, available online at dgii.gov.do or at the airport, though it's often included in your airfare now). Indian passport holders who legally reside in the US (Green Card holders) or hold a valid, unexpired US visa can also enter the Dominican Republic visa-free with just a Tourist Card. OCI cardholders traveling on an Indian passport with a valid US visa or Green Card get the same treatment. If you hold an Indian passport with no valid US, Canadian, UK, or Schengen visa, you will need a Dominican visa from a consulate. All travelers must fill out the electronic entry/exit form (E-Ticket) at eticket.migracion.gob.do before arrival.

**Currency:** Dominican Peso (DOP). Current exchange rate is approximately 60–62 DOP per USD. At all-inclusive resorts, you won't need local currency at all — everything is covered. Outside the resorts, USD is widely accepted in tourist areas but pesos get better rates. Withdraw from Banco Popular or Banreservas ATMs for the best rates. Avoid exchanging money at the airport. Tipping in USD is welcome — $1–2 per service at the resort keeps things flowing smoothly, even at all-inclusive properties.

**Connectivity:** Altice and Claro are the main carriers. Buy a SIM at the airport for $10–15 with data. Most resorts include Wi-Fi. Coverage is reliable in Punta Cana and Santo Domingo but spotty in rural areas and the mountains. T-Mobile includes DR roaming; check your plan before traveling.

**Safety:** Punta Cana's resort zone is very safe — these are gated, patrolled properties and the area caters almost exclusively to tourists. Santo Domingo requires more urban street sense, particularly after dark. Use authorized taxis, avoid displaying expensive jewelry, and stay in well-lit tourist areas. Don't drink the tap water anywhere in the DR — all resorts use purified water for drinking, cooking, and ice, but confirm when eating at local restaurants. Motorcycle taxis (motoconchos) are common but risky — stick to four-wheeled transport.

**Health:** The DR is in a dengue-prone region. Use insect repellent with DEET, especially at dawn and dusk. Bring basic medications from the US — pharmacies exist but selection varies. Sunburn is the most common tourist ailment — the Caribbean sun is significantly stronger than it feels, particularly between 11 AM and 3 PM. Reef-safe sunscreen is both environmentally responsible and increasingly required at snorkel sites.

**Weather & Packing:** Punta Cana is warm year-round (78–88°F). Peak season is December through April — dry, sunny, and slightly cooler with ocean breezes. Rainy season runs May through November, with brief afternoon showers that rarely disrupt a full day. Hurricane season peaks August through October, though the DR's eastern position in the Caribbean means it's hit less frequently than Jamaica or the Bahamas. Pack light, breathable clothing, reef-safe sunscreen, a wide-brimmed hat, and a light rain layer. Bring a dressy casual outfit if you plan to visit the specialty restaurants at your all-inclusive — many enforce a "no shorts, no flip-flops" dress code at dinner.
