## Why Bali?

Bali has quietly become the Indian diaspora's favourite Southeast Asian escape — close enough for a long weekend from Singapore, exotic enough to feel like a proper holiday from the US, and cheap enough to live lavishly on a moderate budget. The island's Hindu culture creates an immediate sense of familiarity for Indians: the temples, the offerings, the incense, the mythology. Bali's version of Hinduism diverged from the Indian mainland centuries ago, making it a fascinating mirror — recognisable but distinctly different. For NRI families, it offers the rare combination of cultural depth, beach luxury, and adventure activities in a single compact destination.

## Top Experiences

**1. Uluwatu Temple at Sunset** — Perched on a 70-metre cliff above the Indian Ocean, this 11th-century sea temple hosts a nightly Kecak fire dance as the sun drops. The setting is theatrically perfect. Watch your belongings — the resident monkeys are professional thieves. Arrive by 4:30 PM for good seats.

**2. Ubud Rice Terraces & Monkey Forest** — Tegallalang's cascading rice terraces are Bali's most iconic landscape. Walk through in the early morning before the Instagram crowds arrive. Then visit the Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary in Ubud — 700 macaques roam through ancient temple ruins draped in banyan trees.

**3. Mount Batur Sunrise Trek** — A pre-dawn hike to the summit of an active volcano. You start at 3 AM and reach the crater rim by sunrise, where your guide cooks eggs in volcanic steam. The panorama over Lake Batur and Mount Agung is worth every step. Book through a local guide association, not a resort markup.

**4. Seminyak Beach Clubs** — Potato Head, Ku De Ta, and La Brisa define Bali's stylish daytime scene. Infinity pools, DJs, cocktails, and Balinese-fusion cuisine against an ocean backdrop. The crowd is young, international, and design-conscious. Reserve a daybed on weekends.

**5. Tirta Empul Water Temple, Tampaksiring** — A holy spring temple where Balinese Hindus come for ritual purification. Visitors can participate — wear a sarong, follow the sequence of fountains, and immerse yourself in spring water that has been considered sacred for over a thousand years. For Hindu NRIs, this is a profoundly moving experience.

## Getting There

Bali's Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) receives direct flights from Singapore (2.5 hours), Kuala Lumpur (3.5 hours), and seasonal routes from Delhi and Mumbai. From the US, connect through Singapore on Singapore Airlines, Tokyo on ANA, or Hong Kong on Cathay Pacific. IndiGo has launched direct Delhi–Bali flights that run during peak season. Within Bali, hire a private driver for about $35–50 per day — it is the most practical way to navigate the island's narrow, traffic-clogged roads.

## Where to Stay

Ubud is for culture and rice terraces; Seminyak and Canggu for beaches and nightlife; Uluwatu for surf and cliffs; Nusa Dua for family resorts. Luxury villas with private pools start at $150 per night — absurd value by global standards. The Four Seasons at Sayan and Mandapa by Ritz-Carlton are top-tier. Mid-range boutique hotels in Ubud run $50–100. Budget rooms and hostels in Canggu are $15–30. Long-stay digital nomad apartments in Canggu cost $500–800 per month. The villa rental market is mature — Airbnb and local agencies offer excellent options.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders receive a free 30-day visa on arrival (or pay $35 for a 60-day extendable e-visa). Carry US dollars for the visa-on-arrival counter. The Indonesian Rupiah is the currency — approximately 13,000 IDR per US dollar. ATMs are widespread but charge fees; carry backup cash. Grab (the Southeast Asian ride-hailing app) works in tourist areas but faces resistance from local taxi cartels. Bali is majority Hindu but the rest of Indonesia is Muslim — dress modestly when visiting non-tourist areas. Water is not potable. Traffic in South Bali is genuinely terrible — budget twice the time Google Maps suggests. The rainy season (November–March) brings afternoon downpours but mornings are usually clear.
