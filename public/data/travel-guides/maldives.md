## Why Maldives?

The Maldives has become the default luxury beach escape for India's affluent diaspora — and for good reason. It is close (four hours from Delhi), requires no visa, and delivers the kind of turquoise-water, overwater-villa fantasy that Instagram was practically invented to showcase. For NRIs celebrating anniversaries, honeymoons, or milestone birthdays, the Maldives offers a controlled luxury experience with none of the logistical friction of a broader India trip. It is also one of the few tropical destinations where you can genuinely unplug — no sightseeing itinerary, no temple queues, just ocean.

## Top Experiences

**1. Overwater Villa Stay** — The defining Maldives experience. Glass floors to watch reef sharks glide beneath you, private decks with direct ocean access, and sunsets that look digitally enhanced. Splurge on at least one night even if budget is tight — the memory pays dividends for decades.

**2. Snorkelling with Manta Rays, Baa Atoll** — UNESCO Biosphere Reserve where manta rays gather between June and November. Hanifaru Bay is the single best manta snorkelling site on earth. Resorts in the atoll offer daily boat trips during season — no diving certification required.

**3. Malé Fish Market & Hukuru Miskiiy** — Skip the resort bubble for a morning. Malé's fish market at dawn is a sensory explosion. Then visit the 17th-century Hukuru Miskiiy (Old Friday Mosque), built from coral stone with intricate lacquer work. It provides context that the resorts deliberately erase.

**4. Sunset Dolphin Cruise** — Nearly every resort offers an evening dhoni cruise to spot spinner dolphins. They travel in pods of fifty or more, leaping and spinning alongside the boat. Bring a good camera and skip the drone — it disturbs them.

**5. Private Sandbank Picnic** — Your resort can arrange a transfer to an uninhabited sandbank — literally a strip of white sand in open ocean with a cooler of champagne and a snorkel set. It is comically extravagant and unforgettable.

## Getting There

Direct flights from Delhi (4 hours), Mumbai (4.5 hours), Bangalore, and Kochi land at Malé's Velana International Airport. IndiGo and Air India operate daily services. From the US, connect through Delhi or via Dubai on Emirates. From Malé, resorts arrange speedboat or seaplane transfers — seaplanes operate only in daylight, so plan your arrival accordingly. Budget an additional $300–600 per person for these transfers depending on distance.

## Where to Stay

The Maldives is essentially a collection of resort islands. Ultra-luxury options include Soneva Fushi, One&Only Reethi Rah, and St. Regis Vommuli — expect $1,000–3,000 per night. Premium brands like Taj Exotica, Anantara, and Conrad offer excellent quality at $400–800. Budget options exist: guesthouses on local islands like Maafushi and Thulusdhoo run $50–150 per night with beach access, though alcohol is not available on inhabited islands. All-inclusive packages are strongly recommended at resorts — à la carte dining adds up brutally.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders receive a free 30-day visa on arrival. OCI cards work fine. The Maldives is a Muslim country — dress modestly on local islands (no bikinis outside resorts). Resort islands operate under different rules. The currency is Maldivian Rufiyaa but US dollars are universally accepted at resorts. Tips are expected (typically $10–20 per day for villa butler). Mobile coverage is good on Dhiraagu or Ooredoo — buy a tourist SIM at Malé airport. The Maldives is sinking — literally. Rising sea levels threaten the nation's existence. Visit with environmental consciousness: choose resorts with coral restoration programmes and avoid single-use plastic where possible.
