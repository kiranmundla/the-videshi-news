## Why France?

France is the country Americans dream about and Indians argue about. The French can be rude — but the food, the architecture, the art, and that indescribable Parisian atmosphere make every minor slight worth enduring. For NRIs planning a European multi-country trip, France is usually either the start or the centrepiece. Paris alone has enough to fill a week, but the real France — Provence's lavender fields, the Loire Valley's châteaux, Normandy's D-Day beaches, and the French Riviera's glamour — requires you to leave the capital. India and France share a deeper historical connection than most NRIs realise: Pondicherry was French until 1954, and the Alliance Française network has taught French to generations of Indians.

## Top Experiences

**1. Eiffel Tower & Seine River Cruise** — The Eiffel Tower at night, when it sparkles on the hour, is genuinely magical. Skip the summit queue — the second floor view is nearly identical. Follow with a Bateaux Mouches dinner cruise on the Seine. Arrive at Trocadéro for the best tower photo angle.

**2. Louvre Museum** — The world's largest and most visited museum. Do not try to see everything — focus on the Mona Lisa wing (Italian Renaissance), Winged Victory, and the Egyptian antiquities. Budget 3–4 hours minimum. Book timed-entry tickets online.

**3. Provence: Lavender Fields & Villages** — Visit in late June through mid-July when the lavender is in peak bloom. The Luberon villages — Gordes, Roussillon, Bonnieux — are achingly beautiful. Rent a car, buy rosé at a roadside vineyard, and pretend you live here.

**4. Mont Saint-Michel** — A medieval abbey on a tidal island in Normandy. It looks like a fantasy painting and is one of France's most visited landmarks. Go at high tide when the island is completely surrounded by water. Stay overnight after the day-trippers leave for the best experience.

**5. French Riviera: Nice, Monaco & Cannes** — The Promenade des Anglais in Nice, the Casino de Monte-Carlo in Monaco, and the Croisette in Cannes — the Côte d'Azur is European glamour distilled. The beaches are pebbly (not sandy), but the water is Mediterranean blue and the people-watching is unmatched.

## Getting There

Direct flights from the US to Paris CDG on Air France, Delta, United, American, and Norse Atlantic. Flight time is 7–8 hours from the East Coast, 10–11 from the West Coast. Budget $400–800 round-trip. TGV high-speed trains from Paris reach Lyon in 2 hours, Marseille in 3 hours, and Nice in 5.5 hours. For Provence, fly into Marseille. For the Riviera, Nice airport is excellent. France's train network is Europe's best — use it.

## Where to Stay

Paris: Le Marais for charm and central location, Saint-Germain-des-Prés for literary Paris, Montmartre for bohemian atmosphere. Hôtel Plaza Athénée and Le Meurice for palace-level luxury. Hôtel Jeanne d'Arc in Le Marais is a beloved mid-range pick. Nice: Old Town (Vieux Nice) for atmosphere, Promenade for views. Provence: Rent a gîte (country house) — the quintessential Provençal experience. French hotels range from €80 boutique to €600+ palace. B&Bs (chambres d'hôtes) offer charm and breakfast for €70–120.

## Food & the Desi Connection

French cuisine is a religion, and learning its rituals is part of the experience. Breakfast is light — croissant, café crème. Lunch is the main meal — a plat du jour at a bistro costs €12–18 and is always good. Dinner is late (8 PM onwards). Must-try: croque-monsieur, duck confit, ratatouille, crème brûlée, and a proper baguette with Brie. Vegetarians face challenges in traditional French restaurants — cheese, bread, salads, and omelettes are your foundation. Paris has a growing vegetarian scene (Le Potager du Marais, Café Pinson). Indian restaurants exist in Paris's 10th arrondissement (Passage Brady, known as "Little India") — dozens of Tamil and North Indian spots.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa — apply at the French consulate through VFS Global, 6–8 weeks ahead. US citizens get 90 days visa-free. Currency is the Euro. Parisians speak English better than they let on — start with "Bonjour" and they warm up considerably. The French are formal about greetings — always say hello and goodbye in shops. The metro is fast and cheap (€2.15/ride, or buy a carnet of 10). Pickpocketing is endemic on the metro and at tourist sites — keep valuables in front pockets. August is when Paris empties — many shops and restaurants close as Parisians go on holiday. Best months: April to June and September to October. July and August are hot (35°C+) and crowded.
