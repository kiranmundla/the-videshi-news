## Why Aruba?

There is a reason Aruba calls itself "One Happy Island," and after visiting, most NRIs will grudgingly admit the marketing is accurate. Aruba sits outside the hurricane belt — a geological quirk that means it almost never rains, almost never storms, and delivers consistent 82°F sunshine roughly 360 days a year. For desi families who have lost vacations to weather cancellations and spent anxious nights watching tropical storm trackers, Aruba offers something priceless: weather certainty. You book, you go, the sun shows up.

The island is small — 20 miles long and 6 miles wide — which means you can see everything without spending half your vacation in transit. The Dutch-Caribbean culture gives it a distinctly European tidiness that Indians will appreciate: the streets are clean, the infrastructure works, and the tap water is desalinated and perfectly safe to drink. Eagle Beach regularly ranks among the world's best beaches, and unlike many Caribbean islands, Aruba feels genuinely safe. Families walk the hotel strip at night without a second thought. For NRIs who have been to Cancún or Punta Cana and found them a bit too chaotic, Aruba hits the sweet spot between Caribbean warmth and Dutch orderliness.

It is also an excellent choice for multigenerational family trips. The resorts are family-friendly without being kiddie-focused, the beaches are calm and shallow enough for grandparents and toddlers alike, and the dining scene is surprisingly diverse. You will not find authentic Indian restaurants, but the international resort kitchens can usually handle vegetarian requests, and the island's Aruban-Dutch cuisine — think gouda cheese croquettes, fresh seafood, and Dutch pancakes — offers enough variety to keep everyone happy.

## Top Experiences

**1. Eagle Beach** — Consistently voted one of the top ten beaches in the world, and it earns it. The sand is powder-white, the water is calm and impossibly blue, and the beach is wide enough that it never feels crowded even at peak season. The iconic fofoti (divi-divi) trees, bent permanently by the trade winds, are the most photographed trees in the Caribbean. Arrive before 9 AM to claim a palapa (thatched umbrella) — they go fast. Beach chairs at hotel-affiliated sections run $10–25/day.

**2. Arikok National Park** — This rugged desert landscape covering nearly 20% of the island is Aruba's wild side. Hike to the Natural Pool (Conchi), a sheltered swimming hole carved into volcanic rock on the windward coast — accessible by 4x4 or a 45-minute hike. UTV rentals for the park run about $150–200 for a half day. Bring water and closed-toe shoes. The Fontein Cave inside the park has Arawak Indian petroglyphs dating back centuries.

**3. Snorkeling at Arashi Beach & Antilla Shipwreck** — Arashi Beach, at the island's northern tip, has excellent shore snorkeling with sea turtles and tropical fish in shallow, calm water. For wreck diving, the Antilla — a 400-foot German freighter scuttled during WWII — is one of the Caribbean's largest shipwrecks and accessible to both snorkelers (from the surface) and divers. Snorkeling tours run $40–65 per person through operators like De Palm Tours or Red Sail Sports.

**4. California Lighthouse & Alto Vista Chapel** — The California Lighthouse at the island's northwest tip offers sweeping views and gorgeous sunsets. Nearby Alto Vista Chapel, a small yellow chapel built in 1750, sits alone on a hilltop surrounded by cacti and desert — it is remarkably peaceful and photogenic. Both are free to visit and can be combined in a single sunset drive.

**5. Downtown Oranjestad** — The capital city is a charming mix of pastel-colored Dutch colonial buildings, duty-free shopping, and waterfront restaurants. The Renaissance Marketplace and Royal Plaza Mall are the main shopping areas. For a more local experience, walk to the Oranjestad Linear Park along the waterfront. Visit on a Tuesday evening for the weekly Bon Bini Festival featuring local music, dance, and food.

**6. Palm Beach Strip** — The high-rise hotel zone where the Ritz-Carlton, Hyatt, and Marriott cluster. The beach here is narrower than Eagle Beach but the water is equally stunning. After dark, this strip comes alive with restaurants, beach bars, and the casual nightlife scene. Bugaloe Beach Bar is a local favorite for sunset drinks over the water.

**7. Baby Beach** — At the island's southern tip, this shallow, lagoon-like beach is perfect for families with small children. The water barely reaches waist-deep for 100 yards out. Bring your own food and drinks — there is a small snack bar but options are limited. Worth the 30-minute drive from the hotel zone.

## Getting There

Aruba's Queen Beatrix International Airport (AUA) is well-served from the US. Nonstop flights operate from Miami (3 hours on American Airlines, JetBlue, Spirit), New York JFK (4.5 hours on Delta, JetBlue), Newark (5 hours on United), Fort Lauderdale (3 hours on JetBlue, Spirit), Boston (5 hours on JetBlue), Houston (4.5 hours on United), Washington Dulles (4.5 hours on United), Charlotte (4 hours on American), Philadelphia (4.5 hours on American), and Atlanta (4 hours on Delta). Round-trip fares typically range from $350–650 depending on the season and origin city.

Aruba has US Customs and Border Protection preclearance at the airport, which means you go through US immigration before boarding your return flight — no long customs lines when you land back in the States. This is a huge convenience, especially for families with kids.

From the airport, taxis to the Eagle Beach hotel zone cost about $20–25 and to Palm Beach about $25–30. There is no Uber, but taxis are regulated with fixed rates posted at the airport. Rental cars are available from $40–60/day and useful for exploring Arikok National Park and the island's south side, but unnecessary if you are staying in the hotel zone.

## Where to Stay

Aruba's hotel scene splits between the high-rise Palm Beach strip and the low-rise Eagle Beach area. **The Ritz-Carlton, Aruba** on Palm Beach is the island's top luxury property — rooms start around $550/night and climb past $900 in peak season, with impeccable service, a gorgeous pool complex, and a world-class spa. **Hilton Aruba Caribbean Resort** on Palm Beach offers reliable luxury from $350–500/night.

For mid-range with excellent value, **Manchebo Beach Resort** on Eagle Beach is an adults-focused property with a yoga vibe, beachfront location, and optional all-inclusive packages from $300–450/night. **Eagle Aruba Resort & Casino** near Eagle Beach runs $190–380/night with kitchen-equipped suites — great for families who want to cook some meals. **Amsterdam Manor Beach Resort** on Eagle Beach is a charming Dutch-colonial boutique hotel from $180–300/night with a genuinely warm atmosphere.

Budget options include **MVC Eagle Beach** from $170–250/night, and **Aruba Surfside Marina** near downtown Oranjestad from $120–180/night. All-inclusive seekers should look at **Divi Aruba All Inclusive** or **Tamarijn Aruba All Inclusive**, both on Druif Beach — rates run $350–500/night and include meals, drinks, and non-motorized water sports, which can save money for families who would otherwise spend heavily on dining out.

## NRI Tips

**Visa information — read this carefully.** Indian passport holders normally require a Caribbean visa to visit Aruba, applied through the Netherlands Embassy. However, if you hold a valid US Green Card or a valid multi-entry US visa (B1/B2, H1B, L1, F1, etc.), you are **exempt** from the visa requirement. US citizens and OCI card holders traveling on US passports enter visa-free for up to 30 days. If you hold only an Indian passport without a US residency permit or multi-entry US visa, you must apply for a Caribbean visa through the Dutch Embassy in India — allow 2–3 weeks processing time. Carry printed proof of your US visa or Green Card at immigration.

All arriving passengers must complete the **Aruba ED Card** (Embarkation/Disembarkation Card) online within 7 days of travel at edcardaruba.aw. A **$20 sustainability fee** is charged to air arrivals at the airport — this is new and not always included in airline pricing.

Currency is the Aruban Florin (AWG), but US dollars are accepted absolutely everywhere — shops, restaurants, taxis, even street vendors. Credit cards work island-wide. No need to exchange currency.

Phone coverage: T-Mobile US plans generally include Aruba for free roaming. AT&T and Verizon charge international rates. Buy a local SETAR SIM at the airport for about $15–20 with data if your plan does not cover it.

Weather is consistently sunny and dry year-round — temperatures hover around 82–88°F. The trade winds keep it comfortable and mosquito-free, which is a major plus. Pack light, breathable clothing and strong sunscreen. The UV index in Aruba is extreme (often 11+), and sunburns happen fast, especially for South Asian skin tones that do not typically burn in the US.

The island is remarkably safe by Caribbean standards. Petty theft exists (do not leave valuables unattended on the beach), but violent crime is very rare. Tap water is desalinated and safe to drink — one of the few Caribbean islands where this is true.

For vegetarians: Aruba's dining scene is better than most Caribbean islands but still meat-and-seafood-heavy. Resort restaurants accommodate vegetarian requests well. Downtown Oranjestad has a few international restaurants with vegetarian options. Pack familiar snacks for picky eaters.
