## Why Fiji?

For diaspora Indians, Fiji carries a significance no other Pacific island can match. Nearly 40% of Fiji's population is of Indian descent — descendants of indentured labourers brought from Bihar, eastern UP, and South India in the late 1800s. You will hear Hindi spoken in markets, find mandirs dotting the countryside, and eat roti and curry that tastes startlingly like home. It is the only tropical island destination where your cultural identity is not an afterthought but a living part of the national fabric. Beyond the heritage connection, Fiji delivers world-class coral reefs, some of the friendliest people on earth, and a pace of life that makes even Goa feel hectic.

Whether you are tracing your family's indenture history, celebrating a destination wedding, or simply want a South Pacific escape where vegetarian food is never a problem, Fiji is uniquely positioned for NRI travellers. The Coral Coast and Yasawa Islands rival the Maldives at a fraction of the cost, and the cultural duality — Fijian and Indo-Fijian — creates a travel experience you genuinely cannot find anywhere else.

## Top Experiences

**1. Nadi & the Sri Siva Subramaniya Temple** — The largest Hindu temple in the Southern Hemisphere sits right in downtown Nadi, painted in vivid Dravidian colours. Free entry. Visit during morning aarti for the full experience, then explore the Nadi Market where Indo-Fijian vendors sell fresh produce, spices, and the best dal puri you will eat outside of Trinidad.

**2. Yasawa Islands** — A chain of volcanic islands with crystal water, dramatic cliffs, and almost no crowds. Take the Yasawa Flyer catamaran from Denarau ($130 round trip) to reach barefoot luxury resorts. The Blue Lagoon — yes, the real one from the film — is here. Snorkelling is world-class and most resorts include meals.

**3. Coral Coast Snorkelling & Diving** — Fiji is the soft coral capital of the world. The Great Astrolabe Reef near Kadavu is one of the largest barrier reefs on earth. Beginner-friendly dives start at $150 for two tanks. The Beqa Lagoon shark dive ($250) puts you face-to-face with bull sharks — not for the faint-hearted but unforgettable.

**4. Sigatoka Sand Dunes & Heritage Trail** — Fiji's first national park, with archaeological sites dating back 2,600 years. The dunes themselves are hauntingly beautiful at sunset. Nearby, the Kalevu South Pacific Cultural Centre offers a Fijian village experience with lovo feast (earth oven cooking), kava ceremony, and meke dance performances.

**5. Suva — The Capital** — Skip the resort bubble for a day and explore Suva. The Fiji Museum covers both indigenous Fijian and Indo-Fijian history, including the indenture period. The municipal market is enormous. The University of the South Pacific campus is worth a walk. For Indo-Fijian heritage, visit the Suva Sangam Temple and the Indian cultural centres.

**6. Mamanuca Islands Day Trip** — The closest island group to Nadi, perfect for day trips. Cloud 9, a floating bar and pizzeria in the middle of the ocean, is exactly as surreal as it sounds ($25 entry). Castaway Island and Malolo are family-friendly with calm lagoons.

**7. Navala Village** — Fiji's last fully traditional village, where every bure is built with local materials. Located in the Ba Highlands, it requires a 4WD and a guide ($80 day trip from Nadi). Bring a bundle of kava root as sevusevu (gift) when entering the village — your guide will arrange this.

## Getting There

Fiji Airways flies nonstop from SFO and LAX to Nadi (10-11 hours). From other US cities, connect through LAX, Honolulu, or Auckland. Fiji Airways frequently runs sales — $700-900 round trip from the West Coast if you book 3-4 months ahead. From the East Coast, expect $1,000-1,400. Korean Air via Seoul and Qantas via Sydney are alternatives. Nadi International Airport is small and efficient; immigration takes 15-20 minutes. Most resorts arrange airport transfers — confirm before arrival.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** Six Senses Fiji on Malolo Island ($800-1,500/night) is the top property. Kokomo Private Island ($1,200-3,000/night) is ultra-exclusive on the Great Astrolabe Reef. On the mainland, the Intercontinental Fiji Golf Resort & Spa on the Coral Coast runs $250-500/night with an excellent kids' club.

**Mid-range:** Outrigger Fiji Beach Resort ($200-350/night) on the Coral Coast is the best value family resort — huge pool complex, beachfront, included kids' programs. Radisson Blu Resort Fiji ($180-300/night) on Denarau is solid and walkable to restaurants. Mantaray Island Resort in the Yasawas ($150-250/night) offers barefoot charm with decent snorkelling.

**Budget:** Beachside Resort Fiji in Nadi ($50-80/night) is clean and functional. Dorm beds in the Yasawas start at $40/night with meals included at places like Oarsman's Bay Lodge. Airbnb options in Nadi and Suva run $40-100/night.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders get a **4-month visa-free stay on arrival** — one of the most generous visa policies anywhere. No advance paperwork needed. US citizens and Green Card holders also enter visa-free. Currency is the Fijian Dollar (FJD); $1 USD ≈ 2.25 FJD. ATMs are common in Nadi and Suva but scarce on outer islands — bring cash for island hopping. Credit cards work at resorts but not village shops.

Vegetarian food is never a problem — Indo-Fijian cuisine is everywhere. Look for roti shops in Nadi, Lautoka, and Ba. The local chilli sauce is addictive. Kava (yaqona) is Fiji's national drink — expect to be offered it everywhere. It tastes like muddy pepper water but the social ritual is important; accept graciously.

Fiji is 20 hours ahead of US Pacific Time, so jet lag hits hard. Plan a rest day on arrival. The dry season (May-October) is ideal — warm days, cool nights, minimal rain. Cyclone season runs November-April; travel insurance is essential if visiting then. Reef-safe sunscreen is both an ecological and legal consideration. Bring water shoes for reef walks and modest clothing for village visits — cover shoulders and knees, and remove your hat when entering a village.
