## Why Banff & the Canadian Rockies?

The Canadian Rockies are the mountains that make the Himalayas' little brother look like a supermodel. Banff National Park, just 90 minutes from Calgary, delivers alpine scenery so absurdly perfect it looks AI-generated — turquoise glacial lakes, snow-capped peaks reflected in still water, elk grazing on emerald meadows at dawn. For NRIs in the American Midwest and Pacific Northwest, Banff is closer than most US national parks and arguably more beautiful than any of them. Calgary is a 4-hour direct flight from major US cities, and the strong Indian community there means you will find familiar food and friendly faces.

There is a deeper appeal for the diaspora too. Canada's massive Indian population — over 1.8 million — means the infrastructure is NRI-friendly in ways that remote US wilderness destinations are not. Calgary has multiple Indian grocery stores, vegetarian restaurants, and gurdwaras. The drive from Calgary to Banff passes through Canmore, a charming mountain town with surprisingly good dining options. And if you are visiting family in Calgary, Edmonton, or Vancouver, the Rockies are a natural add-on that turns a family visit into a vacation.

## Top Experiences

**1. Lake Louise** — The most photographed lake in Canada, and for good reason. The milky turquoise water is colored by glacial rock flour — the finer the summer sunlight, the more vivid the blue. Arrive before 8 AM to beat the tour buses and get parking (lots fill by 9:30 AM in summer). Walk the flat lakeshore trail to the far end for the classic view. In winter, the frozen lake becomes a natural ice-skating rink.

**2. Moraine Lake** — Many travelers say Moraine Lake is even more stunning than Lake Louise. The Valley of the Ten Peaks reflected in the impossibly blue water is the scene from the old Canadian $20 bill. Access is restricted in summer — you must take a Parks Canada shuttle ($8/person, book online 48 hours in advance). The Rockpile Trail (300 meters) gives you the iconic elevated viewpoint.

**3. Icefields Parkway (Highway 93)** — The 144-mile drive from Lake Louise to Jasper is routinely called the most scenic highway in the world. Glaciers, waterfalls, and wildlife at every turn. Stop at Peyto Lake (short uphill walk to viewpoint), the Columbia Icefield, Athabasca Falls, and Mistaya Canyon. Allow a full day for the drive with stops. Fill up on gas at Saskatchewan River Crossing — it is the only station for 100 miles.

**4. Columbia Icefield Skywalk & Glacier Adventure** — Step onto a glass-floored observation platform jutting 100 feet over the Sunwapta Valley, then ride a massive Ice Explorer bus onto the Athabasca Glacier itself. The combo ticket is C$120/adult. The glacier is retreating fast — it has lost half its volume since 1850. Standing on 300 meters of ancient ice is humbling. Book ahead; walk-ups often sell out by noon.

**5. Johnston Canyon** — A 3.4-mile hike along a canyon trail with catwalks bolted to cliff faces above rushing water. The Lower Falls (1.1 km) is easy for all ages; the Upper Falls (2.7 km) is moderate. In winter, the frozen waterfalls create an ice-climbing playground. Go early morning or late afternoon in summer to avoid crowds. Free with park pass.

**6. Sulphur Mountain Gondola** — The Banff Gondola whisks you 2,281 meters to the summit of Sulphur Mountain in 8 minutes (C$75/adult, C$39/child). The 360-degree views of six mountain ranges are spectacular. Walk the Banff Skywalk boardwalk along the ridge. The summit restaurant, Sky Bistro, serves excellent food with views — reserve ahead.

## Getting There

Fly into Calgary International Airport (YYC), which has direct flights from major US hubs: United from Newark, Houston, Denver, and San Francisco; WestJet from many US cities; Air Canada from virtually everywhere. From Calgary, Banff is a 90-minute drive west on the Trans-Canada Highway. No shuttle bus service is reliable — rent a car. You will need a Parks Canada Discovery Pass (C$11/day or C$72/annual per vehicle) for entry to Banff and Jasper National Parks. In summer, an alternative is flying into Vancouver and doing a road trip through the Rockies from the west — the Sea-to-Sky Highway and Rogers Pass route is spectacular but adds 10 hours of driving.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise (C$600–1,200/night) is the iconic grand railway hotel right on the lakeshore. Fairmont Banff Springs (C$500–900) is the "Castle in the Rockies" with a spa, golf course, and century of history. The Juniper Hotel in Banff (C$350–500) offers a modern boutique alternative with mountain views.

**Mid-Range:** The Moose Hotel & Suites in Banff (C$250–400) is centrally located with rooftop hot pools. In Canmore, the Malcolm Hotel (C$200–350) is newer and quieter than Banff options. Lake Louise Inn (C$180–280) is the most affordable option near the lake.

**Budget:** HI Banff Alpine Centre (C$50–80/bed in dorm, C$150 for private room) is a well-run hostel 3 km from town. Camping in Banff National Park (C$28–40/night) is excellent but sites book the moment they open in January. In Canmore, Basecamp Resorts (C$150–220) offers condo-style units with kitchens.

## NRI Tips

US citizens and green card holders need a valid passport to enter Canada — no visa required for stays under 180 days. Indian passport holders with a valid US visa or green card can apply for an eTA (Electronic Travel Authorization, C$7 online) instead of a full Canadian visa. OCI cardholders with a foreign passport follow their passport country's rules. Currency is Canadian dollars — the exchange rate typically favors the USD (roughly 1 USD = 1.35–1.40 CAD). Credit cards are accepted everywhere. Cell service is good in Banff townsite but drops on the Icefields Parkway and backcountry trails — download offline maps. Summer weather is unpredictable: daytime highs of 20–25°C (68–77°F) but nights drop to 5°C (41°F), and snow is possible any month above treeline. Bring layers, rain gear, and sun protection — UV is intense at altitude. Banff townsite has limited dining, but the Masala Craft Indian restaurant is surprisingly good. Wildlife encounters are real — never approach elk or bears, and carry bear spray on any hike longer than a few kilometers. Peak season is July–August; September offers golden larch colors with fewer crowds.
