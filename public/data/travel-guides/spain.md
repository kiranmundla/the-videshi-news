## Why Spain?

For NRI families plotting their first proper European vacation, Spain is the answer you land on after you have dismissed Paris as too expensive, London as too grey, and Italy as too intimidating to drive in. Spain offers the full European experience — centuries-old architecture, world-class art, Mediterranean beaches, and food that will ruin every tapas restaurant in the US for you forever — at prices that are 30–40% lower than France or the UK. A family of four can eat an outstanding three-course Menu del Día lunch at a proper restaurant for $60–70 total, wine included. Try that in Paris.

Spain also solves the vegetarian problem that plagues NRIs across Europe. While it is not India, Spanish cuisine has a rich tradition of vegetable-forward dishes — patatas bravas, tortilla española (a thick potato-and-egg omelette), gazpacho, pimientos de padrón, pan con tomate, and countless cheese and mushroom dishes. Your vegetarian aunty will not starve here. In Barcelona especially, the international dining scene includes plenty of explicitly vegetarian and vegan restaurants. Compare that to the schnitzel-and-sausage desert of Central Europe.

The country spans dramatically different experiences within a single trip. Barcelona gives you Gaudí's surreal architecture and beach-city energy. Madrid delivers world-class museums and royal grandeur. Andalusia — Seville, Granada, Córdoba — offers Moorish palaces and flamenco that carry echoes of the Islamic architectural traditions familiar to anyone who has visited Hyderabad or Lucknow. The Alhambra in Granada, with its intricate geometric patterns and water gardens, will remind Indian visitors of Mughal architecture — not a coincidence, given the shared Persian and Islamic design lineage. Spain is Europe, but it is the corner of Europe that shares the most DNA with the subcontinent.

## Top Experiences

**1. Sagrada Familia, Barcelona** — Gaudí's unfinished masterpiece has been under construction since 1882 and is targeted for completion around 2026. The interior, flooded with colored light from the stained glass, is genuinely transcendent — even if you have seen a thousand cathedral photos, nothing prepares you for standing inside it. Tickets are €26 ($28) and **must** be booked online weeks in advance — walk-ups are turned away. Book the earliest morning slot (9 AM) for the best light and smallest crowds. Tower access costs an extra €10.

**2. The Alhambra, Granada** — A Moorish palace complex that rivals the Taj Mahal for sheer architectural beauty. The Nasrid Palaces, with their honeycombed muqarnas ceilings and reflecting pools, are a direct architectural cousin to Mughal design. Tickets are €19 ($21) and sell out weeks ahead — book at alhambra-patronato.es as soon as you confirm your Spain dates. Seriously, this is the one attraction in Spain that will be sold out if you wait. Your timed entry to the Nasrid Palaces is strict — miss your slot and you lose it.

**3. Prado Museum, Madrid** — One of the world's greatest art museums, housing Velázquez's Las Meninas, Goya's Black Paintings, and Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights. General admission is €15 ($16), but entry is free Monday–Saturday from 6–8 PM and Sundays from 5–7 PM. The museum is enormous — focus on the Velázquez and Goya rooms if you are short on time. Allow 3–4 hours for a meaningful visit.

**4. La Rambla & Gothic Quarter, Barcelona** — Barcelona's most famous boulevard runs from Plaça de Catalunya to the waterfront. The adjoining Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic) is a labyrinth of medieval streets, hidden plazas, and tiny tapas bars. Walk La Rambla once for the experience, then escape into the Gothic Quarter where the real magic lives. **Watch your belongings** — pickpocketing on La Rambla is the most common tourist crime in Spain. Use a crossbody bag and keep phones in front pockets.

**5. Flamenco in Seville** — An authentic tablao (flamenco venue) in Seville is visceral in a way that no video can capture. The passion, the footwork, the raw vocals in a small room — it hits differently than anything in the performing arts. **Tablao El Arenal** and **La Casa del Flamenco** in Seville offer nightly shows from €20–40 ($22–44). Book the dinner-and-show combo for the full experience. Madrid's **Corral de la Morería** is the capital's most famous tablao if you can not make it to Seville.

**6. Park Güell, Barcelona** — Gaudí's colorful hilltop park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, offers mosaic-covered terraces and panoramic city views. The Monumental Zone (the main mosaic area) requires tickets at €10 ($11), booked online. The surrounding park is free and excellent for a morning walk. Visit early (8:30 AM opening) before tour buses arrive.

**7. Royal Palace of Madrid** — The largest functioning royal palace in Europe, though the royal family does not actually live here. The lavish state rooms, the Royal Armory, and the palace gardens are impressive. Tickets are €16 ($17). Monday–Thursday from 4–6 PM (October–March) and 5–7 PM (April–September), entry is free for EU and Latin American citizens — but the lines are long during free hours.

## Getting There

Spain is one of the best-connected European destinations from the US. **Nonstop flights to Madrid** (MAD) operate from New York JFK (7–7.5 hours on Iberia, American, Delta, LEVEL), Newark (7.5 hours on United, Iberia), Miami (9 hours on Iberia, American), Chicago (9 hours on Iberia, American), Dallas-Fort Worth (10 hours on American), and Los Angeles (11 hours on Iberia). **Nonstop flights to Barcelona** (BCN) operate from New York JFK (7.5–8 hours on Delta, LEVEL, United), Newark (8 hours on United), Miami (9.5 hours on American), San Francisco (11 hours on United, LEVEL), and Chicago (9 hours on American, United). Round-trip economy fares typically range from $500–900 from the East Coast and $600–1,100 from the West Coast.

Within Spain, the **AVE high-speed train** is the best way to move between cities. Madrid to Barcelona takes 2.5 hours ($50–120), Madrid to Seville takes 2.5 hours ($40–80), and Madrid to Málaga takes 2.5 hours ($35–70). Book through Renfe.com — advance tickets are significantly cheaper than walk-up fares. The trains are punctual, comfortable, and faster than flying when you factor in airport time.

For an ideal Spain itinerary, fly into Barcelona, take the AVE to Madrid, then train south to Andalusia (Córdoba, Seville, Granada), and fly out of Málaga — covering the country's highlights without backtracking.

## Where to Stay

Spain offers outstanding hotel value by European standards. In **Barcelona**, **Grand Hyatt Barcelona** is the top luxury option from $300–420/night. **Hotel 1898** on La Rambla is a gorgeous boutique hotel in a converted tobacco company headquarters from $200–350/night. **Royal Ramblas** is a solid mid-range choice on La Rambla itself from $180–230/night with a rooftop pool. Budget travelers should look at **Arc la Rambla** from $150–200/night or apartment rentals in Eixample and Gràcia neighborhoods from $80–130/night.

In **Madrid**, **Barceló Torre de Madrid** on Plaza de España is a striking luxury option from $250–300/night. **Hyatt Regency Hesperia Madrid** on Paseo de la Castellana runs $200–280/night. **Hotel Riu Plaza España** is a popular mid-range choice on Gran Vía from $180–230/night with rooftop city views. **Only YOU Boutique Hotel** in Chueca is a stylish design hotel from $250–380/night. Budget options in Madrid start around $80–120/night for clean hotels in the Sol and La Latina neighborhoods.

In **Andalusia**, Seville's boutique hotels and converted palaces offer extraordinary value — expect $100–200/night for charming properties in the Santa Cruz quarter. Granada hotels near the Alhambra run $80–150/night. Spain has an excellent network of **paradores** — historic castles, monasteries, and palaces converted into government-run hotels. They are a unique experience from $120–250/night.

## NRI Tips

**Schengen visa is required for Indian passport holders.** Apply at the Spain VFS Global center at least 3–4 weeks before travel — processing typically takes 10–15 business days. You will need a confirmed flight itinerary, hotel bookings, travel insurance (mandatory, minimum €30,000 coverage), bank statements showing sufficient funds, and an employer letter. The visa fee is €80 ($87). If you already have a valid multi-entry Schengen visa from a previous trip to any EU country, you can enter Spain on it. **US citizens and OCI card holders with US passports** enter Spain visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Starting 2025, the EU's **ETIAS** (European Travel Information and Authorization System) requires US passport holders to register online before travel — it is a simple online form ($8 fee), not a visa, but do it before you fly.

Currency is the Euro (€). As of mid-2026, €1 ≈ $1.08 USD. Credit cards (Visa and Mastercard) are widely accepted, but carry some cash for small tapas bars, markets, and tipping. ATMs are everywhere — use bank-affiliated ATMs to avoid fees. Tip modestly — rounding up or leaving 5–10% at restaurants is standard. Spain does not have the US tipping culture.

**Pickpocketing is the number one tourist crime.** Barcelona's La Rambla, Madrid's Puerta del Sol, and crowded metro stations are hotspots. Use a crossbody bag with a zip closure. Keep your phone in your front pocket. Do not set bags down at restaurant tables — use a purse hook. This is not fearmongering; it is the single most common issue affecting tourists in Spain.

Phone coverage: most US plans offer international roaming in Spain, but data charges vary. Buy a local SIM from Vodafone, Orange, or Movistar at any electronics store for €10–20 with generous data. Free Wi-Fi is available at most hotels, cafes, and train stations.

**For vegetarians**: Spain is more accommodating than most European countries. **Patatas bravas** (fried potatoes with spicy sauce), **tortilla española** (potato omelette), **gazpacho** (cold tomato soup), **pimientos de padrón** (blistered peppers), **pan con tomate** (bread with tomato), and **queso manchego** (sheep's milk cheese) are all naturally vegetarian and available everywhere. Barcelona has numerous dedicated vegetarian and vegan restaurants — **Teresa Carles** and **Flax & Kale** are excellent. In smaller Andalusian towns, options narrow — learn to say "sin carne, sin pescado" (without meat, without fish) and you will manage.

**Meal timing is critical.** Spaniards eat lunch at 2–3 PM and dinner at 9–10 PM. Restaurants are often closed between 4–8 PM. Adjust your schedule or you will find yourself hungry and shut out. The **Menu del Día** (daily set menu) offered at lunch — typically a starter, main, dessert, bread, and a drink for €12–18 — is the best value meal in Europe and available at most sit-down restaurants.

**Weather**: Spain is not uniformly warm. Barcelona and the coast are Mediterranean (hot summers, mild winters). Madrid is on a high plateau — scorching in July/August (100°F+) and surprisingly cold in January. Andalusia is the warmest region year-round. The ideal visit window is **April through June** or **September through October** — comfortable temperatures, fewer crowds, and lower prices than the July–August European holiday peak.
