## Why Egypt?

Egypt is the civilization trip — the one that makes you feel small in the best possible way. Standing at the base of the Great Pyramid of Giza, you are looking at a structure that was already 2,000 years old when Cleopatra was born, already ancient when the Roman Empire fell. For Indian families, Egypt holds a particular fascination: it is one of the few civilizations that rivals India's own antiquity, and the parallels are striking — the reverence for the Nile mirrors the Ganges, the temple complexes of Luxor echo Hampi and Khajuraho, and the mathematical precision of the pyramids shares DNA with Vedic geometry. Your kids will learn more in a week in Egypt than in a semester of history class.

Egypt is also remarkably affordable by American standards. A luxury Nile cruise, five-star hotels, private guides, and all entrance fees can be had for what a mid-range European trip costs. The country has invested heavily in tourism infrastructure since the 2020s, with the Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM) near Giza — the largest archaeological museum in the world — finally opening as a world-class facility. For NRIs who have hesitated about Egypt due to safety or hassle concerns, the tourism experience has dramatically improved. Visa on arrival is straightforward, Uber works in Cairo, and English-speaking guides are everywhere.

## Top Experiences

**1. Pyramids of Giza & the Sphinx** — No amount of photos prepares you for the scale. The Great Pyramid stands 481 feet tall and was the tallest structure on earth for 3,800 years. Arrive at opening (8 AM) and start with the Panoramic Viewpoint for the classic three-pyramid shot. Entry to the Giza plateau is EGP 540 ($11). Going inside the Great Pyramid costs an additional EGP 800 ($16) — claustrophobic but unforgettable. Hire an official guide at the gate ($30–50 for 2 hours) and firmly decline touts selling camel rides.

**2. Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM)** — The newly opened museum near Giza houses over 100,000 artifacts, including the complete Tutankhamun collection — all 5,000+ items from his tomb, most never before displayed. The museum is a architectural marvel in itself. Full-day tickets are EGP 1,000 ($20) for the standard galleries; the Tutankhamun halls require a separate ticket (EGP 1,500/$30). Allow at least 4–5 hours. This alone justifies a trip to Egypt.

**3. Luxor — Valley of the Kings & Karnak Temple** — Luxor is the world's greatest open-air museum. On the West Bank, the Valley of the Kings holds 63 royal tombs — your basic ticket (EGP 600/$12) includes entry to three tombs. The tomb of Seti I is the most spectacular (separate ticket, EGP 1,800/$36). On the East Bank, Karnak Temple's Hypostyle Hall — 134 columns, each 70 feet tall — is one of the most awe-inspiring spaces ever built. A hot air balloon at sunrise over the West Bank ($80–120) is the iconic Luxor experience.

**4. Nile River Cruise (Luxor to Aswan)** — A 3–4 night cruise between Luxor and Aswan is the classic Egypt experience. The ship docks at temples along the way — Edfu (the best-preserved temple in Egypt), Kom Ombo (a double temple on a Nile bend), and Philae Temple in Aswan. Luxury cruises (Oberoi Philae, Sanctuary Sun Boat) run $300–500/night. Mid-range options (Movenpick, Sonesta) are $100–200/night including meals and guided tours. Sailing a traditional felucca is a budget alternative for the Aswan stretch.

**5. Abu Simbel** — Ramesses II's colossal temple, carved into a cliff face, with four 66-foot seated statues of the pharaoh guarding the entrance. The temple was relocated piece by piece in the 1960s to save it from the Aswan Dam's rising waters — an engineering feat nearly as impressive as the original construction. Abu Simbel is 280 km south of Aswan — fly (30 minutes, $120–180 round trip) or join a convoy bus ($30–40, 3 hours each way, departs 4 AM). The dawn arrival means you see the temple in magical early light.

**6. Islamic Cairo & Khan el-Khalili** — The medieval heart of Cairo, with mosques, madrasas, and the famous Khan el-Khalili bazaar — one of the oldest markets in the Middle East, operating continuously since 1382. Explore Al-Muizz Street (the most beautiful street in Cairo), visit the Al-Azhar Mosque, and bargain for brass lanterns, spices, and papyrus in the souq. Expect to bargain hard — start at 30% of the asking price.

## Getting There

EgyptAir operates nonstop flights from New York JFK and Washington Dulles to Cairo (CAI), approximately 11 hours eastbound. Round-trip fares run $700–1,100. Alternatively, connect through European or Gulf hubs: Emirates via Dubai, Turkish Airlines via Istanbul, or Lufthansa via Frankfurt all serve Cairo with competitive fares ($600–900). Cairo International Airport has improved significantly — Terminal 2 is modern and efficient. From the airport, Uber to central Cairo costs EGP 200–350 ($4–7). Domestic flights to Luxor and Aswan are frequent and cheap ($60–100 one way on EgyptAir or Nile Air).

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** The Marriott Mena House in Giza ($250–500/night) has pyramid views from its pool and garden — one of the most iconic hotel settings in the world. In Luxor, the Sofitel Winter Palace ($200–400) is a colonial-era grande dame where Howard Carter stayed during the Tutankhamun excavation. On the Nile, the Oberoi Philae cruise ($350–500/night) is the finest floating hotel in Egypt.

**Mid-Range:** The Kempinski Nile Hotel in Cairo ($120–200) offers 5-star riverside elegance at mid-range prices. In Luxor, Steigenberger Nile Palace ($80–150) has a prime Corniche location. Aswan's Mövenpick Resort ($100–180) sits on Elephantine Island in the middle of the Nile.

**Budget:** Cairo's Le Meridien Pyramids ($60–100) is a solid airport-adjacent option with distant pyramid views. In Luxor, Bob Marley House ($15–30) is a popular backpacker guesthouse with a rooftop overlooking the Nile. Aswan's Keylany Hotel ($25–40) offers clean rooms near the Corniche. Budget travelers can find Nile felucca overnight trips from Aswan for $30–50/person including meals.

## NRI Tips

US passport holders can obtain a visa on arrival at Cairo airport ($25, single entry, valid 30 days) — the process is simple: buy a visa sticker from the bank kiosk before passport control. E-visa is also available online at visa2egypt.gov.eg ($25, processed in 5–7 days). Indian passport holders must apply for a visa in advance through the Egyptian embassy or consulate ($25–60, 5–10 business days). OCI card holders follow their passport country's rules. Currency is the Egyptian Pound (EGP); 1 USD ≈ 49–51 EGP. Exchange money at banks, not street changers. Cash is essential — many smaller sites, restaurants, and taxi drivers do not accept cards. Egypt is overwhelming at first — touts, noise, traffic, and the heat can be intense. Use Uber in Cairo (it works well), never agree to a tour without fixing the price first, and firmly say "la, shukran" (no, thank you) to touts. Baksheesh (tipping) is expected everywhere — carry small bills (EGP 20–50). Modest dress is essential at mosques; women should carry a scarf. Summer (June–August) is extremely hot — Luxor and Aswan regularly exceed 45°C. Visit October–March for comfortable temperatures. Stomach issues are common — drink only bottled water, avoid raw salads at budget restaurants, and carry Imodium. Vegetarian food is available but limited — falafel, ful medames (fava beans), koshari, and fattoush are the staples. The Egyptian Museum and sites are open during Ramadan but with reduced hours.
