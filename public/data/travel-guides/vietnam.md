## Why Vietnam?

Vietnam is the destination that keeps showing up in NRI travel group chats — and for good reason. It is jaw-droppingly beautiful, extraordinarily cheap, and serves some of the most exciting food in Asia. For Indian Americans used to spending $300 a night in Europe, Vietnam offers comparable scenery and cultural depth at a quarter of the price. The country has modernised rapidly but still feels authentically Southeast Asian in a way that overdeveloped destinations like Singapore or Bangkok no longer do. Ha Long Bay alone is worth the trip. Add Hoi An's lantern-lit streets, Hanoi's chaotic old quarter, and Saigon's war-history-meets-skyscraper energy, and you have a country that delivers far more than its modest price tag suggests.

## Top Experiences

**1. Ha Long Bay Overnight Cruise** — Take a two-day, one-night junk boat cruise through 1,600 limestone karsts rising from emerald water. Kayak through sea caves, swim at hidden beaches, and wake up to mist-covered islands. Book a mid-range operator — $150–200 per person includes everything.

**2. Hoi An Ancient Town** — A UNESCO World Heritage Site with Japanese bridges, Chinese temples, and French colonial architecture. Get clothes custom-tailored in 24 hours — Hoi An is famous for it. The lantern festival on the 14th of each lunar month turns the town into a floating light show.

**3. Hanoi Old Quarter & Street Food** — The 36 streets of Hanoi's Old Quarter are organised by trade — silk street, silver street, paper street. Eat bún chả (the dish Obama ate with Anthony Bourdain), drink egg coffee at Cafe Giang, and navigate the organised chaos on foot.

**4. Ho Chi Minh City: War Remnants Museum & Cu Chi Tunnels** — Regardless of your politics, these are profoundly moving. The War Remnants Museum should be a mandatory stop. Cu Chi Tunnels are a 90-minute drive from the city — crawl through the tunnels the Viet Cong used.

**5. Ninh Binh: Tam Coc Boat Ride** — Called "Ha Long Bay on land," the Tam Coc boat ride through rice paddies and limestone caves is ethereally beautiful. Less touristy than Ha Long and just 90 minutes from Hanoi. Combine with Bai Dinh Pagoda, Southeast Asia's largest Buddhist temple complex.

## Getting There

From the US, fly into Hanoi (Noi Bai Airport) or Ho Chi Minh City (Tan Son Nhat) via Seoul, Tokyo, or Taipei. Korean Air via Incheon is often the best value. Flight time is 18–22 hours with one stop. VietJet and Bamboo Airways run cheap domestic flights between Hanoi, Da Nang (for Hoi An), and Ho Chi Minh City — $30–60 one way. The north-to-south journey covers 1,700 km — fly between cities, explore locally.

## Where to Stay

Hanoi: The Old Quarter for atmosphere (Silk Path Hotel, Hanoi La Siesta), West Lake for modern luxury (InterContinental). Hoi An: Stay inside the ancient town at boutique properties — An Bang Beach area for beach proximity. Ho Chi Minh City: District 1 for central access (Park Hyatt, Liberty Central). Vietnam's hotel value is extraordinary — four-star boutique hotels cost $40–80/night. Five-star runs $100–180. Budget hostels are $8–15.

## Food & the Desi Connection

Vietnamese food is lighter and fresher than Indian food, but equally obsessed with herbs, chillies, and complex broths. Phở (beef noodle soup), bánh mì (baguette sandwiches), bún chả (grilled pork with noodles), and gỏi cuốn (fresh spring rolls) are all must-tries. Vegetarians: Vietnamese Buddhist cuisine (cơm chay) is widespread — look for restaurants with the Buddhist swastika symbol. Hanoi and HCMC have small Indian communities with restaurants serving North Indian food. Indian instant noodles and snacks are available at convenience stores — the cultural exchange runs both ways.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport holders need an e-visa — apply online, approved in 3 business days, costs $25 for 30 days. US citizens also need an e-visa (same process). Currency is Vietnamese Dong (VND) — $1 ≈ 25,000 VND. The large numbers take adjustment. ATMs are everywhere; Visa/Mastercard widely accepted in cities. Grab is the ride-hailing app — works for motorbike taxis too (adventurous but efficient). Vietnam is very safe for tourists. The biggest hazard is crossing the street in Hanoi — walk steadily, do not stop or run, and traffic flows around you. Best months: March to May and September to November. June to August is hot and humid. December to February is cool in the north, pleasant in the south.
