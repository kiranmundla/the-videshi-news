## Why Hawaii?

Hawaii is the domestic vacation that feels genuinely foreign — and for NRIs living on the US mainland, that combination is irresistible. No passport, no visa, no currency exchange, no jet lag (from the West Coast), but volcanic landscapes, Polynesian culture, and water so clear it feels computer-generated. Hawaii also has a significant Indian community — there are Hindu temples on Maui and Kauai, Indian restaurants in Honolulu, and a multicultural heritage that makes brown families feel welcome in a way that some mainland beach towns do not. It is the one vacation where every generation — grandparents, parents, kids — finds something to love.

## Top Experiences

**1. Nā Pali Coast, Kauai** — By boat, helicopter, or the Kalalau Trail, the Nā Pali Coast is Hawaii's most dramatic scenery. Towering emerald cliffs plunging into sapphire ocean. A zodiac boat tour ($200–250) combines cliff views with dolphin spotting and sea cave entry.

**2. Haleakalā Sunrise, Maui** — Watch the sunrise from 10,023 feet above the clouds inside a dormant volcanic crater. Reserve a spot in advance (required by the National Park Service). Bring layers — it is 30–40°F at the summit even in summer.

**3. Waikiki Beach & Diamond Head, Oahu** — The most famous beach in the Pacific. Waikiki is perfect for families — gentle waves, warm water, and surfing lessons everywhere. Hike Diamond Head crater in the morning (1.6 miles round trip) for panoramic views of Honolulu.

**4. Volcanoes National Park, Big Island** — Walk across a live volcanic landscape. The Kīlauea caldera, steam vents, and lava tubes are otherworldly. If an eruption is active (check NPS.gov), the night-time glow is something you will never forget.

**5. Snorkelling at Molokini Crater, Maui** — A partially submerged volcanic crater forms a natural crescent that shelters some of the clearest water in Hawaii. Visibility can reach 150 feet. Morning boat tours from Ma'alaea Harbor are the standard — book early.

## Getting There

Direct flights from the West Coast are 5–6 hours. From the East Coast, 10–11 hours with a connection or direct on Hawaiian Airlines from JFK/BOS (seasonal). Honolulu (Oahu) is the main hub. Inter-island flights on Hawaiian Airlines or Southwest Hawaii cost $60–120 each way. Most NRI families visit two islands in a week — Oahu + Maui or Maui + Big Island is the classic combination. Kauai is less developed and best for nature lovers.

## Where to Stay

Oahu: Waikiki has every chain hotel imaginable. Halekulani and Royal Hawaiian for luxury, Outrigger and Hyatt Regency for mid-range. North Shore for surfers and solitude. Maui: Ka'anapali Beach is the resort strip — Westin, Marriott, Sheraton line the sand. Wailea is more upscale and quieter. Big Island: Kohala Coast resorts (Mauna Lani, Fairmont Orchid) for luxury, Hilo side for volcano access and budget options. Kauai: Princeville for North Shore luxury, Poipu for South Shore family resorts. Vacation rentals (Airbnb/VRBO) offer significant savings, especially for families.

## Food & the Desi Connection

Hawaiian food is a cultural mashup — Polynesian, Japanese, Chinese, Filipino, Portuguese, and American influences create something entirely unique. Must-tries: poke bowls (fresh raw fish over rice), plate lunch (rice + macaroni salad + protein), loco moco (burger patty, egg, rice, gravy), shave ice (not a snow cone — much better), and malasadas (Portuguese doughnuts). Vegetarians will find good options at health-conscious Hawaiian restaurants — açaí bowls, taro dishes, and the farm-to-table movement is strong. Indian restaurants exist in Honolulu — Bombay Palace and Himalayan Kitchen. Hindu temples on Kauai (Kauai's Hindu Monastery) and Maui (Maui Hindu Temple) welcome visitors.

## NRI Tips

No passport or visa needed — Hawaii is a US state. All you need is your ID and a flight. Currency is USD. The biggest consideration is cost — Hawaii is expensive. Budget $250–400/day for a family of four including accommodation, food, and one activity. Save money by cooking some meals (vacation rentals with kitchens help), eating plate lunches instead of resort restaurants, and booking activities directly rather than through the hotel concierge. Sunscreen containing oxybenzone and octinoxate is banned in Hawaii to protect coral — bring reef-safe SPF. Ocean safety: respect the surf, swim only at lifeguarded beaches, and never turn your back on the ocean. The best months are April to June and September to November — slightly lower prices, fewer crowds, and warm water.
