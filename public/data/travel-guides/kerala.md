## Why Kerala?

They call it God's Own Country, and for the diaspora, Kerala is proof that India can be lush, unhurried, and startlingly green. If your memories of India are dusty northern plains, Kerala will reset every assumption. The backwaters, the spice plantations, the Ayurvedic retreats — this is India at its most restorative. For NRIs burnt out by American hustle culture, two weeks in Kerala functions as both holiday and therapy. It is also one of India's safest and most literate states, making it an excellent first-India-trip destination for partners or children who have never visited.

## Top Experiences

**1. Alleppey Houseboat, Backwaters** — Glide through Kerala's famous backwater canals on a traditional kettuvallam. Book a two-bedroom boat with a private cook who will prepare fresh karimeen (pearl spot fish) and appam. The overnight option lets you wake to mist rising off paddy fields.

**2. Munnar Tea Plantations** — Rolling emerald hills carpeted in tea bushes as far as the eye can see. Visit the Tata Tea Museum, then hike to Top Station for panoramic views. The drive up from Kochi is spectacular — plan three hours and stop often.

**3. Periyar Wildlife Sanctuary, Thekkady** — Spot wild elephants from a bamboo raft on Periyar Lake. The border-hiking trail with a tribal guide is the best way to see the forest up close. Leeches are part of the package during monsoon — bring salt and long socks.

**4. Fort Kochi Heritage Walk** — Portuguese churches, Dutch cemeteries, Chinese fishing nets, Jewish synagogues, and spice warehouses, all within walking distance. The Kochi-Muziris Biennale, held every two years, has turned the neighbourhood into a contemporary art hub.

**5. Varkala Cliff Beach** — Red laterite cliffs dropping straight to the Arabian Sea. Far less commercial than Goa, with excellent seafood shacks along the cliff edge. The Janardanaswamy Temple nearby is over 2,000 years old. Visit between November and March for calm seas.

## Getting There

Kochi (Cochin) International Airport receives direct flights from the Middle East and connecting flights from all major Indian cities. From the US, fly to Delhi or Mumbai, then connect to Kochi (2.5 hours) or Trivandrum (3 hours). Emirates and Etihad via Dubai or Abu Dhabi offer competitive one-stop options. Once in Kerala, distances are modest — Kochi to Munnar is four hours, Kochi to Alleppey is 90 minutes. Pre-book a private car with driver through your hotel for the most comfortable experience; it costs roughly ₹3,000 per day.

## Where to Stay

Kerala excels at boutique and Ayurvedic properties. Kumarakom Lake Resort and Coconut Lagoon are world-class luxury on the backwaters. In Munnar, Windermere Estate offers plantation living at mid-range prices. For Ayurvedic immersions, Somatheeram and Kalari Kovilakom are internationally renowned — book weeks-long Panchakarma packages in advance. Budget travellers will find homestays across the state from ₹1,500 per night, often including home-cooked Kerala meals that alone justify the trip.

## NRI Tips

Indian passport and OCI holders enter without visa requirements. Kerala's monsoon season (June–August) brings dramatic rainfall — the backwaters are actually at their most beautiful then, but trekking and beaches are limited. The sweet spot is September to March. UPI is widely accepted, even at small shops. A local SIM from Jio or Airtel costs ₹500 at the airport. Mosquito repellent is essential year-round. Kerala food is spicier than most NRIs expect — if you have been away for years, ease in gently. Alcohol is available but regulated; toddy shops offer the authentic local experience. Dress modestly at temples — many require mundu or full-length clothing.
