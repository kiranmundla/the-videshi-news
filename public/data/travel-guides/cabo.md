## Why Cabo?

For NRIs accustomed to planning trips that require visa applications, time-zone math, and 16-hour flights, Cabo San Lucas is the antidote. It is a two-to-three hour nonstop flight from most West Coast cities, it accepts USD almost everywhere, and the entire tourist corridor operates in a language of sun, tequila, and zero planning stress. This is the trip your friend group has been trying to coordinate for three years — the one where everyone actually shows up because the logistics are laughably simple.

Cabo has quietly become the go-to for NRI bachelor parties, milestone birthdays, and "we all have PTO to burn" weekends. The appeal is obvious: world-class resorts at prices that undercut Hawaii by 30–40%, a nightlife strip along Medano Beach that gets genuinely rowdy, and enough adventure activities — snorkeling, ATVs, deep-sea fishing — to keep the crew from defaulting to poolside arguments about cricket. For couples, the Pacific-side sunsets and fine dining scene rival anything in the Mediterranean. For families, the calmer San José del Cabo side offers art galleries, a quieter beach, and none of the spring-break energy. The beauty of Cabo is that it shapeshifts to match whoever is visiting.

## Top Experiences

**1. El Arco de Cabo San Lucas** — The iconic rock arch at the tip of the Baja Peninsula where the Pacific meets the Sea of Cortez. Take a glass-bottom boat or water taxi from Medano Beach for $15–20 per person. Go early morning for the best light and fewer boats. If you're lucky, you'll spot sea lions lounging on the rocks nearby. Lover's Beach, accessible only by boat, is right next to the arch — bring water shoes and a waterproof phone case.

**2. Snorkeling at Chileno Bay** — Cabo's best easily accessible snorkeling spot, with calm, clear water and abundant tropical fish. The bay is protected, so it's ideal even for beginners. Rent gear on-site for $10–15 or bring your own. Arrive before 11 AM — the bay gets crowded by midday. Pelican Rock, reachable by a short boat ride, is another excellent option with sea fans and rays.

**3. Whale Watching (December–April)** — Humpback and grey whales migrate through the Sea of Cortez every winter, and Cabo is one of the best places on Earth to see them up close. Book a tour with Cabo Adventures or Whale Watch Cabo for $80–150 per person. The smaller zodiac boats get you closer than the large catamarans. A genuinely humbling experience — these animals are the size of city buses and breach within meters of the boat.

**4. Sunset Dinner Cruise** — Multiple operators run sunset cruises along the arch and coastline. The CaboRey catamaran offers an open bar and decent dinner for around $95–120 per person. For something more upscale, book the Jazz & Wine cruise. The golden hour light hitting the desert cliffs from the water is peak Instagram. This is the one activity where the whole group unanimously agrees it was worth it.

**5. Medano Beach Day** — Cabo's main swimmable beach is a scene unto itself. Rent a daybed at Mango Deck or The Office for $30–50 (redeemable on food and drinks). Jet ski rentals run $60–80 for 30 minutes. The beach vendors are persistent but polite — a firm "no gracias" works. The vibe is lively, borderline party, especially on weekends. If you want calm, head to Palmilla Beach instead.

**6. San José del Cabo Art Walk** — Every Thursday evening from November to June, the galleries in downtown San José del Cabo open their doors for a free art walk. Local artists, complimentary wine, live music — it's the cultured counterweight to Cabo's party reputation. The town square and surrounding streets have excellent restaurants. Flora Farms, a farm-to-table restaurant about 15 minutes outside town, is worth the detour — book at least a week ahead.

**7. Deep-Sea Fishing** — Cabo calls itself the "Marlin Capital of the World" for good reason. Charter a boat through Pisces Sportfishing or RedRum Sportfishing starting at $350–500 for a half-day shared charter, or $800–1,500 for a private boat. Striped marlin, yellowfin tuna, and dorado are the main catches. Most operators practice catch-and-release for marlin. Even non-fishers enjoy the boat ride and the open-ocean experience.

## Getting There

Nonstop flights to Los Cabos International Airport (SJD) run from nearly every major US city. From LA and San Francisco, expect 2.5–3 hours on Alaska Airlines, United, or Delta. From Dallas, American Airlines has daily nonstops at around 3 hours. From Houston, Southwest and United offer 2.5-hour flights. From New York JFK, JetBlue and Delta fly nonstop in about 5.5 hours. Denver, Chicago, and Phoenix also have direct service. Round-trip fares range from $250–500 depending on season — book early for winter dates (December–March is peak).

From the airport, the resort corridor stretches between San José del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas (about 20 miles apart). Most resorts offer airport shuttles for $25–40 per person each way, or you can arrange a private transfer through SJD Taxi for $60–80 for the car. Renting a car is useful if you plan to explore beyond the corridor, but unnecessary for a resort-focused trip. Uber is available but limited — stick to the official transport.

## Where to Stay

**Luxury:** The Waldorf Astoria Los Cabos Pedregal ($800–1,200/night) is the crown jewel — a tunnel carved through rock leads to a secluded beach, and the rooms have private plunge pools. Las Ventanas al Paraíso by Rosewood ($1,000–1,500/night) is similarly extraordinary, with butler service and telescopes in every room for stargazing. Chileno Bay Resort by Auberge Resorts ($500–800/night) is slightly more accessible while still firmly in the exceptional category.

**Mid-Range:** The Hard Rock Hotel Los Cabos All-Inclusive ($400–550/night) is popular with groups — the all-inclusive format means nobody argues over the bill. Pueblo Bonito Sunset Beach ($280–350/night all-inclusive) has six pools and a gorgeous clifftop setting. Casa Dorada Los Cabos ($200–280/night) on Medano Beach is perfectly located for a walkable, lively trip.

**Budget:** Marina Fiesta Resort & Spa ($150–200/night) is centrally located by the marina with solid amenities. Hotel Riu Palace Cabo San Lucas ($250–330/night all-inclusive) offers strong value on Medano Beach. For true budget stays, downtown Cabo San Lucas has clean hotels from $60–100/night, though you'll sacrifice beach access.

## NRI Tips

**Visa:** US citizens fly in with just a passport — no visa needed, just the immigration form handed out on the plane. Indian passport holders with a valid, unexpired US visa stamp or Green Card enter Mexico visa-free for up to 180 days. OCI card holders traveling on an Indian passport must have either a valid US visa stamp or Green Card. If your US visa stamp is expired (even with a valid I-797), you will need a Mexican visa — apply at the nearest Mexican consulate, which costs $54 and requires bank statements showing financial solvency. Do not book flights before confirming your visa situation.

**Currency:** Mexican Pesos are the official currency, but Cabo is the most dollar-friendly destination in Mexico. Resorts, restaurants, tours, and taxis all accept USD. That said, you'll get a slightly better deal paying in pesos — withdraw from ATMs at Santander or HSBC (avoid the Euronet machines, which charge absurd fees). Credit cards are widely accepted. Your Indian debit card may work at ATMs, but carry a US bank card as backup.

**Connectivity:** Buy a Telcel SIM at the airport or at any OXXO convenience store for about $10–15 with generous data. If you have T-Mobile or Google Fi from the US, your plan likely includes free roaming in Mexico. WhatsApp works perfectly for coordinating with the group.

**Safety:** The tourist corridor between San José del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas is safe and heavily patrolled. Use common sense — don't flash expensive jewelry, avoid deserted areas at night, and use official taxis or hotel transport. The tap water is not safe to drink anywhere in Mexico; stick to bottled water, and be cautious with ice outside of resorts.

**Weather:** Cabo is a desert — summers (June–September) are hot and humid with temperatures above 95°F, and September is hurricane season. The ideal window is November through May, with warm days, cool evenings, and virtually no rain. December through March is peak season, so expect higher prices and crowded resorts. Pack reef-safe sunscreen (regular sunscreen is banned at many snorkel sites), a light cover-up for sun protection, and comfortable walking shoes for San José del Cabo's cobblestone streets.
