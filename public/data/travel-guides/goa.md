## Why Goa?

For the Indian diaspora, Goa occupies a peculiar place in the imagination — part Bollywood beach fantasy, part Portuguese colonial curiosity, part hippie legend. The reality is better than all three. Goa is India's smallest state and its most laid-back, a place where Catholic churches sit beside Hindu temples, where the fish curry rivals anything in Lisbon, and where the pace of life makes even Keralites seem hurried. For NRIs, Goa works brilliantly as a decompression stop: fly in from the US, spend three days on a beach getting over jet lag, then tackle the rest of India refreshed.

## Top Experiences

**1. Old Goa Churches** — The Basilica of Bom Jesus, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, holds the remains of St. Francis Xavier. Se Cathedral next door is the largest church in Asia. The Portuguese Baroque architecture is unlike anything else in India — a genuine culture shock within the country.

**2. Palolem Beach, South Goa** — A crescent of sand lined with colourful beach huts and coconut palms. South Goa is quieter, cleaner, and more family-friendly than the north. Kayak to Butterfly Island in the morning, eat fresh kingfish at a shack for lunch, nap in a hammock by afternoon.

**3. Fontainhas Latin Quarter, Panaji** — Narrow streets lined with pastel-painted Portuguese houses, art galleries, and bakeries selling bebinca. Walking through Fontainhas feels like Lisbon transplanted to the tropics. The Goa State Museum nearby provides colonial-era context.

**4. Dudhsagar Falls** — A four-tiered waterfall plunging 310 metres through lush jungle on the Goa-Karnataka border. Best visited during monsoon (July–September) when the falls are at full force. Take the jeep safari from Mollem — the approach through the Bhagwan Mahavir Wildlife Sanctuary is half the experience.

**5. Saturday Night Market, Arpora** — A sprawling bazaar with live music, international food stalls, handicraft vendors, and a party atmosphere. It runs only on Saturdays from November to April. Arrive at sunset and eat your way through — the lamb kebabs and wood-fired pizza are both excellent.

## Getting There

Goa's Dabolim Airport (GOI) connects directly to Mumbai (1 hour), Delhi (2.5 hours), Bangalore, and Hyderabad. The new Manohar International Airport at Mopa handles additional traffic from northern cities. From the US, connect through Mumbai or Delhi. Goa is also well served by the Konkan Railway — the train from Mumbai follows a spectacularly scenic coastal route (12 hours, worth it for the views). Within Goa, rent a scooter for maximum freedom or use local taxis.

## Where to Stay

Goa's accommodation ranges from ₹800 backpacker hostels to ₹80,000 luxury resorts. The Taj Exotica and W Goa in South Goa set the luxury standard. For mid-range charm, boutique Portuguese heritage homes in Fontainhas or Assagao offer far more character than chain hotels — Elsewhere and SaffronStays curate excellent options from ₹5,000 per night. Beach huts in Palolem and Agonda (₹1,500–3,000) are the quintessential Goa experience. Book early for December and January, when prices triple and availability vanishes.

## NRI Tips

No visa needed for Indian passport or OCI holders. Goa's peak season is November to February — pleasant weather, packed beaches. Monsoon (June–September) is dramatic and uncrowded; many beach shacks close but the interiors are stunning. UPI works everywhere. Goa is more relaxed about dress and alcohol than most of India, but respect local sensibilities at churches and temples. Tap water is not potable. Scooter rentals are cheap (₹300–500/day) but verify insurance and always wear a helmet — Goa's roads are narrow and unpredictable. The Goa Police do conduct checks, so carry your driving licence.
