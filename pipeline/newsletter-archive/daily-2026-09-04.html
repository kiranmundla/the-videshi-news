ISRO Targets Gaganyaan Uncrewed Launch by Late 2026 as Space Calendar Fills Up
  • ISRO is targeting Q4 2026 for the Gaganyaan G-1 mission, India's first uncrewed orbital test flight, which will carry the Vyommitra half-humanoid robot to validate life-support and crew systems in low Earth orbit.
  • The National Review Committee cleared the G-1 mission for launch in May 2026, with campaign activities requiring a 90-day preparation window for the complex human-rated systems.
  • Three uncrewed flights (G-1, G-2, G-3) are planned before India's first crewed mission, now targeted for 2027-2028, with a total budget allocation of Rs 20,193 crore.
  • The timeline follows a programme reset after a January 2025 internal review found earlier launch targets were premature and system readiness was insufficient.

India's Gaganyaan programme is on track for its first uncrewed orbital test flight by the end of 2026, according to government statements and programme documents, as ISRO prepares to validate the spacecraft and systems that will eventually carry Indian astronauts into space.

The G-1 mission, targeted for Q4 2026, will send the Gaganyaan crew module into low Earth orbit at approximately 400 km altitude carrying Vyommitra, a half-humanoid robot designed to simulate astronaut conditions and provide critical data on life-support and environmental control systems.

Clearance After Programme Reset

The National Review Committee granted approval for the G-1 mission in May 2026, according to Indian Defence News, with campaign activities set to begin 90 days before lift-off. The extended preparation window reflects the complexity of human-rated launch vehicle systems, all of which are being developed indigenously — significantly longer than the 60-day preparation period typical for standard satellite launches.

The clearance came after what officials acknowledged was a turbulent period.
