Modi Signs a Strategic Partnership in Auckland. Outside the Arena, 300,000 Indian Kiwis Are Living Through a Racism Crisis.
Key Takeaways
  • Modi's visit marks the first by an Indian PM to New Zealand in 40 years.
  • A free trade agreement promises tariff-free access for 57% of New Zealand's exports to India.
  • Anti-Indian sentiment is rising, with incidents of racism reported among the Indian community.
  • 292,000 Indians live in New Zealand, making up 5.5% of the population.
  • Concerns grow that increased migration may exacerbate anti-immigrant sentiment.
The optics were historic. On Saturday in Auckland, Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and his New Zealand counterpart Christopher Luxon elevated their bilateral relationship to a "strategic partnership" — a diplomatic upgrade reflecting, in official language, "shared democratic values, deep people-to-people links, and shared interests in the Indo-Pacific." It is the first visit by an Indian prime minister to New Zealand in 40 years.
