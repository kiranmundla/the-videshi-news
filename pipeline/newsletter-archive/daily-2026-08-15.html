The '40-Year-Old ICU Club': Why Doctors Are Warning Against Sacrificing Sleep for Success
  • India ranks as the world's second most sleep-deprived nation, trailing only Japan, with 59-61% of Indian adults getting less than six hours of uninterrupted sleep according to 2026 data.
  • ICMR data shows sudden cardiac deaths among people under 40 have risen by more than 40% in five years, with 64% of cases involving chronic sleep deprivation as a major factor.
  • Dr Navin Gnanasekaran of Apollo Hospitals coined the term "40-year-old ICU club" to describe the surge of otherwise healthy professionals ending up in intensive care with preventable metabolic and cardiac events.
  • With 101 million diabetics, 136 million prediabetics, and 315 million people with hypertension across India, sleep deprivation compounds an already severe metabolic crisis.

A Term No One Wants to Belong To

Dr Navin Gnanasekaran, Head of Radiology at Apollo Hospitals Chennai and author of "The 100 Year Blueprint: An Indian Doctor's Guide to Longevity," has a name for a pattern he sees with increasing frequency: the "40-year-old ICU club." It refers to professionals in their 40s — often high-earning, gym-going, seemingly healthy — who end up in intensive care with heart attacks, strokes, or acute metabolic crises that could have been prevented.

Speaking on India Today's "Doctor Vs Internet" programme, which has surpassed 140,000 views since its August 1 publication, Gnanasekaran laid out a case that India's obsession with hustle culture and sleep sacrifice is producing measurable health damage at a population level.

India's Sleep Deficit in Numbers

Fitbit data ranks India as the second most sleep-deprived nation in the world, behind only Japan. Indians average seven hours and one minute of sleep per night, but the distribution is sharply skewed.
