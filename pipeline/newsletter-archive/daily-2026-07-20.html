Singapore Airlines Deepens Role at Air India as Losses Cross $3 Billion
  • Singapore Airlines has moved executives into Air India's flight operations, engineering, and maintenance functions, escalating from strategic partner to hands-on operational presence.
  • Air India posted an estimated net deficit of $3 billion in the financial year ending March 2026, worsening from $2.4 billion the prior year.
  • SIA CEO Goh Choon Phong and Tata Group Chairman Natarajan Chandrasekaran have met to discuss a funding road map and the search for a new CEO after Campbell Wilson's resignation.

Singapore Airlines is taking a far more active role inside Air India, deploying its own executives into critical operational positions as India's flag carrier battles record financial losses, safety lapses, and the departure of its chief executive.

SIA has placed its people in key roles across flight operations, engineering, and maintenance at Air India in recent months — areas where the Singaporean carrier has long been considered a global benchmark, according to people familiar with the matter.

A Turnaround Under Strain

The deeper involvement marks a notable escalation since a deadly Boeing 787 Dreamliner crash in June 2025 that killed more than 240 passengers and plunged Air India into its worst crisis. What began as a strategic partnership has evolved into a far more hands-on presence, with SIA now effectively running core operational functions while the Tata Group — which owns 74.9 percent of Air India — focuses on commercial, human resources, finance, and IT.

Air India reportedly approached Tata Sons and Singapore Airlines for at least $1.14 billion (INR 10,000 crore) in additional funding after posting losses of $2.4 billion in the financial year ending March 2025.
