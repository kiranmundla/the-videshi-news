Viral Hepatitis Remains a Silent Threat as India Pushes for Elimination by 2030
  • India has screened over 21 crore people and treated more than 6 lakh patients under its National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme, now in its eighth year.
  • Chronic hepatitis B and C infections cause no symptoms for years but can progress to cirrhosis and liver cancer if left untreated.
  • Kerala recorded over 31,000 hepatitis A cases and 82 deaths in 2025 alone, highlighting ongoing sanitation and waterborne transmission challenges.
  • India's hepatitis B birth-dose vaccination coverage has crossed 92%, but only about 65% of pregnant women are currently screened.

India's Elimination Push Gains Ground

India marked World Hepatitis Day 2026 on July 28 under the global theme "Hepatitis: Let's Break It Down," with the Union Ministry of Health reaffirming its commitment to eliminating viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030. Eight years into the National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (NVHCP), the country now operates 1,153 treatment centres — exceeding the original target of one per district — and has screened more than 21 crore beneficiaries, according to data shared by Union Health Secretary Punya Salila Srivastava.

"India is among the few countries providing free-of-cost treatment for Hepatitis C and management of Hepatitis B, while continuing to improve access and affordability of services," Minister of State for Health Anupriya Patel said at the event.
