Both Parents' Vitamin B12 Levels Linked to Birth Defect Risk, Major Study Finds
  • A prospective cohort study of over 3,000 couples and 17,765 women found that higher vitamin B12 levels in both parents before conception were associated with a lower risk of birth defects.
  • The study, published in the Annals of Internal Medicine, is among the first to quantify the role of paternal B12 status in preventing birth defects — shifting the focus beyond maternal nutrition alone.
  • The findings carry particular weight for India, where vitamin B12 deficiency affects an estimated 40–75% of the population, driven largely by vegetarian and near-vegetarian diets.
  • India has one of the world's highest rates of neural tube defects, with an estimated 115,000 preventable cases annually, according to prior population-based studies.

A Study That Changes the Equation

A large prospective study from China has found that higher vitamin B12 levels in both parents around the time of conception are associated with a significantly lower risk of birth defects — a finding that extends the conversation about preconception nutrition well beyond mothers.

https://x.com/CaVivekkhatri/status/2095010476870820113

Researchers from the Children's Hospital of Fudan University and colleagues used the Shanghai Preconception Cohort to study 3,032 couples who provided blood samples within four months before conception and 17,765 women tested at four months' gestation or earlier. The study measured vitamin B12 and red blood cell (RBC) folate levels and examined their relationship with birth defects identified among live births, stillbirths and pregnancies ending because of fetal abnormalities.

The results, published in the Annals of Internal Medicine, showed that higher preconception maternal and paternal vitamin B12 levels were both associated with a lower predicted prevalence of birth defects.
