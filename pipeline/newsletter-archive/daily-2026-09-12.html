DHS Proposes $103,265 Fee on Every H-1B Cap Petition in Sweeping Rulemaking
  • The Department of Homeland Security has proposed a $103,265 fee on every H-1B cap-subject petition, on top of all existing filing fees, estimating it would collect $8.8 billion a year.
  • The fee would apply to all 85,000 annual cap petitions — including the 20,000 advanced-degree exemption and F-1 students changing status inside the U.S. — but not to cap-exempt petitions from universities and nonprofit research organizations.
  • The rule is legally distinct from the 2025 executive-order $100,000 H-1B fee, which a federal court vacated in June 2026 and which only applied to consular processing abroad.
  • If finalized, the fee would apply prospectively to fiscal year 2028 petitions (calendar year 2027), with significant legal challenges expected.

The Department of Homeland Security is proposing to charge employers $103,265 for every H-1B petition filed under the annual cap — a fee that would dwarf all existing immigration filing costs and fundamentally change the economics of hiring foreign specialty workers.
