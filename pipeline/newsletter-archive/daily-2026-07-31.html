DR Congo's Ebola Death Toll Passes 1,500 as Outbreak Becomes Second-Largest on Record
  • The DRC's Ebola outbreak has killed 1,556 people out of 3,532 confirmed cases in under three months, making it the second-largest Ebola epidemic on record.
  • The rare Bundibugyo strain driving the outbreak has no approved vaccine or treatment, though clinical trials for two therapies began in early July.
  • Africa CDC estimates $1.4 billion is needed to contain the epidemic, but only $213.9 million has been secured so far.
  • A U.S. travel ban is restricting the flow of aid workers into the DRC, forcing organisations like Samaritan's Purse to scale back operations.

Fastest-Spreading Ebola Epidemic in History

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak is now the world's second-largest on record, government data released on Friday showed, with 3,532 confirmed cases and 1,556 deaths since the epidemic was declared on May 15.

Only the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak — which recorded 28,616 cases and 11,310 deaths across Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone — was larger, according to the World Health Organization.
