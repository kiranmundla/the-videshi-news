US Makes Visa Bond Program Permanent, Raises Maximum to $20,000 — India Not on List
  • The US has made its visa bond pilot program permanent, raising the maximum bond from $15,000 to $20,000 for B1 and B2 visa applicants from 50 countries.
  • India is not on the list of affected countries, but South Asian neighbors Bangladesh, Nepal, and Bhutan are included.
  • The final rule, published in the Federal Register on August 3, eliminates the $5,000 bond option, leaving $10,000, $15,000, and $20,000 as the three tiers.

The US State Department has made its visa bond program permanent, according to a Federal Register notice published on Friday. The final rule, which takes effect today, August 3, raises the maximum bond to $20,000 and applies to citizens of 50 countries seeking B1 (business) or B2 (tourism) visas.

"Consular officers may require covered nonimmigrant visa applicants to post a bond of up to $20,000 as a condition of visa issuance, as determined by the consular officers," the notice said, according to Reuters.

From Pilot to Permanent

The program began as a pilot in August 2025 under the Trump administration, initially offering consular officers three bond options: $5,000, $10,000, or $15,000.
