USS Abraham Lincoln Passes 240 Days at Sea Without Port Call During Iran Deployment
  • USS Abraham Lincoln has been at sea for approximately 244 consecutive days without a port call, shattering the previous modern US Navy record of 206 days
  • Reports of food shortages and poor meal quality aboard the carrier drew national attention in April, though Navy leadership firmly denied the claims
  • The carrier's 5,000-plus crew has not had a port call since a brief stop in Guam on December 11-12, 2025 — normal deployments include port visits every 30-45 days
  • The extended deployment supports Operation Epic Fury against Iran, with more than 50,000 US military personnel in the Middle East

The USS Abraham Lincoln has now spent approximately 244 consecutive days at sea without a single port call, obliterating the modern US Navy record and raising mounting questions about crew welfare as America's sole supercarrier near Iran continues combat operations with no end in sight.

The Nimitz-class aircraft carrier, carrying more than 5,000 sailors and Marines, departed Naval Base San Diego on November 21, 2025, for what was originally a routine Indo-Pacific deployment. After a brief two-day stop in Guam on December 11-12, the ship was redirected to the Middle East and has not made another port call since — a stretch now approaching nine months at sea.
