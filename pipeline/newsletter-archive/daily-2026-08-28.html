🛂 IMMIGRATION — TOP STORY
H-1B Crackdown Widens as Trump Administration Targets OPT, Fees and Vetting Rules
  • The Department of Homeland Security has proposed making the H-1B visa fee permanent at $103,265, up from the initial $100,000 executive order fee set to expire in September
  • A separate $100,000 fee on Optional Practical Training (OPT) — the program that lets international students work after graduation — is pending White House review
  • H-1B registrations have dropped more than 25% from 2024, with employers seeking about 344,000 visas compared to 794,000 in 2023
  • Hospitals, research institutions, and nonprofits would be exempt from the new fee under the proposed rule

The Trump administration is broadening its crackdown on work visa programs, moving beyond the initial H-1B fee increase to target the Optional Practical Training program, tighten applicant vetting, and propose making six-figure visa fees a permanent fixture of the US immigration system.

In a rule proposed this week, the Department of Homeland Security would set the H-1B visa fee at $103,265 — a permanent replacement for the $100,000 fee imposed by executive order last year, which is set to expire in September. The government projects the new fee would generate roughly $8.8 billion in revenue to fund the immigration system, including immigration courts that handle deportation cases.

OPT in the Crosshairs

The administration is also considering a $100,000 fee for Optional Practical Training, the program that allows international students to work in the US for up to three years after graduation on their student visas.
