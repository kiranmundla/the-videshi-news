North Korean Missile Unit Deploys to Russia With Up to 120 Ballistic Missiles for Ukraine Strikes
  • A North Korean missile unit of about 90 personnel has begun deploying to Russia's Voronezh region, potentially equipped with up to 120 ballistic missiles and six launchers, according to Ukrainian military intelligence.
  • The deployment follows a deadly North Korean missile strike on the Ukrainian village of Radushne that killed at least five members of a single family — the first use of North Korean missiles in Ukraine since August 2025.
  • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned in July that Russia wants to bring in 30,000 additional North Korean troops, with preparations already underway in the Voronezh region.
  • Around 9,500 North Korean troops remain in Russia's Kursk region from a 2024 deployment, though they are not currently directly involved in operations against Ukraine.

A North Korean missile unit has begun deploying to western Russia and could be equipped with up to 120 ballistic missiles and six launchers for strikes against Ukraine, a Ukrainian military intelligence official said, marking a significant escalation of Pyongyang's involvement in Europe's largest conflict since World War II.

The disclosure came days after a North Korean ballistic missile flattened a residential home in the central Ukrainian village of Radushne, killing at least five members of the Voronov family — the first confirmed use of North Korean missiles in Ukraine since August 2025.

What We Know About the Deployment

Andrii Cherniak of Ukraine's Main Directorate of Intelligence told Reuters that Russia planned to base the missile unit of approximately 90 North Korean personnel within its 112th Missile Brigade in the Voronezh region. Pyongyang has already shipped a batch of 40 KN-23 and KN-24 missiles along with personnel, but the full configuration — expected to include 120 missiles and six launchers — will be finalized in top-level talks next month.
