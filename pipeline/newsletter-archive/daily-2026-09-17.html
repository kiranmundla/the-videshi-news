Supreme Court Demands Answers on 34 Unnatural Deaths in Manipur Relief Camps
  • The Supreme Court on Thursday directed Manipur's chief secretary to file an affidavit explaining the deaths — especially unnatural deaths — of internally displaced persons living in relief camps.
  • The directions followed a Justice Gita Mittal committee report recording 34 cases of unnatural death in the camps, of which only 25 were registered as FIRs and post-mortems were conducted in just 20.
  • The bench questioned why families of those who died received only Rs 20,000 to Rs 30,000 in compensation, and ordered the state's legal services authority to ensure an FIR in every case of unnatural death.
  • A report placed before the court referred to 640 deaths in relief camps across eight districts; the next hearing is on October 5, 2026.

The Court's Questions

Expressing shock over the deaths, a bench comprising Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana asked why the state had taken no significant steps on information furnished by the Justice Gita Mittal committee about deaths — including unnatural ones — of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in relief camps.
