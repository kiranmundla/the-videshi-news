The Odyssey Review: Nolan's Greek Epic Is a Spectacle Worth the Voyage — Mostly

By Editor

  • Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey is a sweeping, nearly three-hour adaptation of Homer's epic poem, shot entirely on IMAX 70mm cameras across seven countries with a $250 million budget.
  • Ludwig Göransson's score is the film's standout — a pulse-pounding soundscape that elevates every sequence, even on a regular screen.
  • The film excels at drawing newcomers into Greek mythology, making audiences curious about characters and stories they may never have encountered.
  • Some island adventures drag, and the pacing dips in stretches where spectacle alone can't sustain momentum — but these are minor blemishes on an otherwise grand achievement.

Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey is the kind of movie Hollywood rarely makes anymore — a sprawling, R-rated epic adapted from one of the oldest stories in human history, asking audiences to sit with mythology rather than rush through it. Two weeks into its theatrical run, it has already grossed over $720 million worldwide, opened to $264 million globally (Nolan's biggest debut ever), and become the largest live-action opening of 2026.
