iPhone 18 Pro Could Cost $300 More as Memory Chip Shortage Drives 38% Build Cost Surge
  • The iPhone 18 Pro could start at $1,399, up $300 from the current iPhone 17 Pro, as Apple faces a 38% increase in component costs driven by surging memory chip prices.
  • Memory now accounts for roughly 34% of iPhone component costs, up from 10% a year ago, as AI data center demand creates a global DRAM and NAND shortage.
  • Apple CEO Tim Cook called the memory price surge "a hundred-year flood," and the company has already raised prices on Macs, iPads, Apple TV, HomePod, and Vision Pro.
  • Apple may absorb part of the increase by switching to its own C2 modem and cutting non-memory component costs, potentially limiting the consumer-facing hike to $50-$100.

Apple's next iPhone lineup will cost significantly more to build, and consumers will feel at least part of the impact. Multiple analyst reports now estimate that the iPhone 18 Pro's bill of materials will rise 38% over its predecessor, with soaring memory chip prices responsible for the bulk of the increase.

The Memory Math

According to TrendForce's latest analysis, memory accounted for about 10% of the iPhone Pro's total component cost a year ago.
