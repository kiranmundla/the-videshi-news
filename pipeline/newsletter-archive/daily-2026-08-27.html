Why September 3 Is a Pivotal Date for International Students on F-1 and J-1 Visas
  • A federal court hearing on September 3, 2026 will consider a preliminary injunction that could block the DHS rule ending "duration of status" for F-1 and J-1 visa holders before it takes effect on September 15.
  • A coalition of universities, unions, and advocacy groups sued DHS on August 18, arguing the rule could disrupt degree completion, physician training, and trigger economic harm if international enrollment falls.
  • The new rule caps F and J visa stays at four years, requires USCIS extension applications with biometric screening and background checks, and cuts the F-1 post-completion grace period from 60 to 30 days.
  • Major universities including Harvard, Columbia, Ohio State, and Georgetown are urging international students to return to the US before September 15 to avoid being readmitted under the new fixed-term system.

The September 3 Court Hearing

For the roughly 1.5 million international students and exchange visitors currently in the United States on F and J visas, September 3 has emerged as a critical date. A federal court is scheduled to hear arguments that day on a preliminary injunction that could block — or allow — a sweeping new Department of Homeland Security rule from taking effect on September 15.

A coalition of universities, labor unions, and immigrant advocacy groups filed suit against DHS on August 18, seeking to halt the regulation.
