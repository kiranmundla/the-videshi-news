Texas Mayor Joins Growing Push Against H-1B Use by Public Entities
  • League City Mayor Nick Long said "no public entity should be using the H-1B program" and that "taxpayers should get taxpayer-funded jobs," responding to Texas State University's filing for a PR specialist role
  • Governor Abbott froze new H-1B petitions at all Texas state agencies and public universities in January 2026, requiring written approval from the Texas Workforce Commission through May 2027
  • Texas public universities sponsor roughly 1,200 H-1B visa holders, with UT Southwestern Medical Center (228), Texas A&M (214), and MD Anderson (171) among the largest users
  • Indians make up an estimated 71% of all approved H-1B applications nationally, making the Texas restrictions directly relevant to the diaspora community

Mayor's Public Opposition

Nick Long, the Republican mayor of League City, Texas, has publicly opposed the use of H-1B visas by public entities, adding a local voice to the growing political pressure on the program in the state.

Long's comments came in response to a post on X announcing that Texas State University had filed a notice of intent to sponsor an H-1B visa for a Public Relations Specialist position. Scott Bowen, the Republican nominee for the Texas House of Representatives, shared the filing and questioned whether none of the university's own communications graduates were qualified for the role.

Long quoted Bowen's post and wrote: "No public entity should be using H-1B program.
