# Enola Holmes 3 Review: Millie Bobby Brown's Detective Is All Dressed Up With Nowhere Thrilling to Go

Netflix's third outing for the youngest Holmes trades London fog for Maltese sunshine — and a murder mystery for a wedding that nobody RSVPed for.

---

There is a particular kind of Netflix film that feels engineered in a lab — gorgeous locations, likeable cast, just enough plot to keep you swiping past the "Are you still watching?" prompt. *Enola Holmes 3* is exactly that film. It is a sun-drenched, costume-rich, aggressively charming confection that goes down easier than a glass of Maltese limoncello and leaves roughly the same impression: pleasant, forgettable, and gone by morning.

Millie Bobby Brown returns as literature's most caffeinated teen detective, now operating her own agency in London and absolutely thriving in the girlboss era of Victorian problem-solving. When her mother Eudoria (Helena Bonham Carter, collecting a paycheque with the elegance of someone who once played the Queen Mother) summons the family to Malta for what appears to be a wedding, Enola smells something fishier than the Valletta harbour. She's right, naturally. Sherlock (Henry Cavill, still looking like he was carved from marble and then taught to brood) gets himself kidnapped within the first act, and suddenly the wedding dress becomes a crime scene.

Director Philip Barantini — yes, the man behind *Adolescence* and *Boiling Point*, two of the most viscerally intense British productions of recent years — seems like an odd choice for a YA romp, and he is. Barantini brings competence but little of the raw nerve that made his earlier work crackle. The shift from one-take kitchen nightmares to a sunny caper about a teenage girl solving crimes in petticoats is, to put it generously, a lateral move. To put it honestly, it's a waste.

## The Malta of It All

The film's genuine ace is its setting. Malta has never looked this ravishing on screen — the honey-coloured limestone of Mdina's silent city, the cerulean waters off Comino, the baroque grandeur of St. John's Co-Cathedral doubling as a villain's lair. Cinematographer Matthew Lewis shoots the island like a love letter, and costume designer Consolata Boyle (*The Queen*, *Florence Foster Jenkins*) delivers gowns that deserve their own Instagram account. If nothing else, *Enola Holmes 3* will do for Malta's tourism board what *White Lotus* did for Sicily.

But spectacle without substance is just a screensaver, and the plot here is thinner than the paper Enola's coded messages are written on. Jack Thorne's screenplay juggles a kidnapping, a forged painting, a secret society, and a wedding — and drops most of them. The mystery is so signposted that you'll solve it during the opening credits. The romance between Enola and Tewkesbury (Louis Partridge, earnest and slightly lost) remains the franchise's least convincing subplot, a will-they-won't-they with all the tension of a pre-approved mortgage.

## New Faces, Old Problems

The additions to the cast offer flickers of life. Himesh Patel brings a warm, slightly bumbling energy to Dr. Watson — a welcome departure from the character's usual straight-man drudgery. Sharon Duncan-Brewster's Moriarty is formidable and underused, a villain who deserves a film of her own rather than fifteen minutes of scheming in someone else's. Both actors suggest a richer, darker franchise lurking somewhere beneath the YA surface, but the film isn't brave enough to go there.

And that's the central problem. Three films in, *Enola Holmes* remains trapped in a liminal space — too clever for kids, too sanitised for adults, too formulaic for anyone who's read the source material. Brown is genuinely good in the role; her direct-to-camera asides have sharpened from cute to confident, and she handles the physical comedy with real skill. But even she seems to be running on autopilot by the third act, as if the script has stopped surprising her as much as it's stopped surprising us.

## The Streaming Verdict

Critics have been politely divided — the film sits at 68 per cent on Rotten Tomatoes with a 72 per cent audience score, the cinematic equivalent of a participation trophy. "A fine caper," said *Empire*, in the tone one uses to describe a restaurant where the food was fine and you'd never go back. *Collider* was blunter: "The franchise seems to have run out of steam."

For the diaspora audience streaming from Houston to Hyderabad, *Enola Holmes 3* is a perfectly serviceable Tuesday-night watch — the kind of film you put on while folding laundry and occasionally glance up when Cavill appears shirtless (he doesn't, but you keep hoping). It won't offend, it won't challenge, and it certainly won't linger.

Sometimes a detective story solves everything except the mystery of why it exists. *Enola Holmes 3* is streaming now on Netflix. Bring low expectations and a glass of wine, and you'll be just fine.
