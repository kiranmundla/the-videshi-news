What: Russian drone strikes on the Ukraine-Moldova border crossing and Kyiv, killing two at the border and one in the capital. When: Overnight Tuesday into Wednesday, September 9. Where: Starokozache crossing, Odesa region; Kyiv. Why it matters: Moscow is systematically targeting Ukraine's land trade routes while pressure builds on neighbouring Moldova and Romania.

Russian drones struck the Starokozache border crossing between Ukraine and Moldova on Wednesday, killing two civilians and injuring three, Ukrainian and Moldovan officials said, in the latest sign that Moscow is expanding its campaign against Ukraine's trade and logistics routes. The strike sparked a major fire and forced authorities to suspend passenger and vehicle traffic at the crossing, which sits about a kilometre from Moldovan territory and is used daily by thousands of people and hundreds of cars.

Odesa regional governor Oleh Kiper said the two victims were civilians near the crossing. Sixteen people were evacuated from the area, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who urged world leaders to respond more forcefully to Moscow's continued attacks.

A pattern of attacks on border crossings

The Starokozache strike was not an isolated incident. Ukrainian border guards said Russia has struck five border crossings in recent weeks — four along the border with Moldova and one along the border with Romania, a NATO member.

"Most likely, Russia wants to disrupt logistics routes," Andriy Demchenko, spokesperson for the Ukrainian border guards, told national television.

Kyiv has become increasingly reliant on land routes for its exports as Russian attacks on Black Sea ports have disrupted maritime trade. The repeated targeting of crossings with Moldova — a non-NATO neighbour — and Romania raises the risk of the war spilling further across Ukraine's western borders.

Kyiv faces renewed assault

The capital came under heavy attack overnight and into Wednesday. Russia targeted Kyiv with jet-powered drones, causing multiple fires and disrupting businesses and schools, city officials said. One person was killed and 14 wounded, according to the city's military administration. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said a drone struck a 16-storey apartment building early on Wednesday, sparking a fire that emergency crews extinguished.

Russia resumed its near-constant air attacks on Kyiv on Tuesday after a brief halt during the visit of US peace negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Ukrainian officials said five people had been killed in the capital in Tuesday's attacks. Zelenskyy called the three rounds of talks with the US delegation substantive and expressed hope they would revive negotiations; Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Moscow welcomed Trump's efforts.

"A response is needed — Moscow must not go into the winter believing that it can get away with missile and drone terror without consequences."

— Volodymyr Zelenskyy, President of Ukraine

Moldovan airspace violated a day earlier

The border strike followed a separate escalation on Tuesday, when Russian drones violated Moldovan airspace, forcing Chișinău International Airport to temporarily suspend arrivals and departures and delaying Zelenskyy's departure for Norway, where he attended King Harald V's funeral.

Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre said Zelenskyy's plane was "almost hit" by a drone as it prepared to take off. A Moldovan government spokesperson said two Russian drones were involved: one landed in a field north of the capital, sparking a fire, while the other continued into Romanian airspace. Moldova's ambassador to the United States, Vladislav Kulminski, told CNN the incursion was likely "not an accident," noting that Zelenskyy is known to use Moldovan airspace for foreign visits. Peskov claimed the threat to the plane "did not exist."

Moldovan President Maia Sandu pledged measures to protect national airspace and accused the country's pro-Russian opposition of endangering national security by downplaying the threat from Moscow.

Impact and what comes next

For Indian readers, the direct stakes lie in energy and trade: prolonged disruption of Black Sea and overland routes keeps upward pressure on global crude and freight costs, while the safety of the remaining Indian students and workers in Ukraine remains a concern each time strikes intensify.

Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces had retaliated by striking a naval base, an oil terminal and military port facilities in Russia's Novorossiysk. With winter approaching, the question is whether stepped-up sanctions and air-defence support arrive before Russia's campaign against Ukraine's logistics network inflicts lasting economic damage.
